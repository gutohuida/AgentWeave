### The correction R1 had to make to its own first answer

The obvious one-line fix — pass `agent_wide=True` and stop — **breaches a shipped requirement.**
`agent-conversation-workspace`, *A delivery attempt is counted only where a delivery was attempted*
(2026-08-28): *"This SHALL NOT extend to a refusal that prevents only this input from being
delivered. Where other queued input could have run, the input at the head of the queue is in the way,
and the system SHALL go on counting."* A blocked **agent** worktree does not stop a task-bound turn —
that one runs in `.agentweave/tasks/<id>` — and `schedule_agent` builds its turn from the oldest
eligible entry across the agent's whole queue, so an unbound head really does hold a task entry back.
Flagging unconditionally would repair one finding from the 2026-08-28 sweep by breaching a
requirement from the same sweep. Written up as design D4 rather than discovered later.

So: the raise site states the *scope* (`agent_workspace_unavailable`, a new flag, deliberately not a
fourth meaning for `agent_wide`), and `turn_scheduler` — the only party holding the rest of the queue
— answers the requirement's actual question. Reaching that arm proves the agent writes and the
project is a repo, so `takes_task_workspace` reduces to `task_id is not None` for every other entry;
the test is *any entry outside `selected` that names a task or a review, or whose conversation
carries a binding* (`Conversation.task_id`, verified present and indexed at `db/models.py`).
