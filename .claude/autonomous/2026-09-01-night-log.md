### The row's headline question was driven — and the finding it nearly produced was disproved

**An edit to a bound charter reaches the next turn, including a resumed one.** Charter says
`ALPHAq1`, turn 1 replies `ALPHAq1`, `PATCH` to `BETAq1`, turn 2 in the *same* conversation
(`conv-45b7db016716` both times, `--resume` on provider session `bd6c486c-…`) quotes the new
charter back verbatim. `agent_trigger.py:893-895`'s claim now has a drive behind it.

**What almost got filed.** The first version asked turn 2 to *obey* rather than *quote*, and the
agent answered with the **superseded** marker. Its thinking then said, unprompted, *"There are two
charter instructions in my system prompt… 1. ALPHAq1… 2. BETAq1."* That is a specific, structured
report of the system prompt accumulating one ~7.7 kB context block per resumed turn — a real and
expensive defect if true. Three measurements, and the third killed it: the materialized context
file on disk carried only the new text; the Claude session transcript stores no system prompt at
all, so disk could not confirm it either way; and a **three-turn probe** — charter edited before
turn 2 and again before turn 3, then asked to *count* the marker lines — answered `1` /
`WORDTHREE`. One block, the current one.

So the model was reading its own earlier reply, which had quoted the old charter inside a thinking
block. **An agent's self-report about its own system prompt is evidence about the conversation, not
about what the harness delivered.** Two runs agreed with each other and were both wrong about the
mechanism. The harness now asks turn 2 to quote rather than obey, and carries a comment saying why.

originally watched the agent *timeline* for a terminal event, and on turn 2 broke out instantly
because turn 1's `run_completed` was still there — reading the output before turn 2 had produced
anything, and reporting the product broken when it was not. Polling is now per-`run_id`.
