Summary

What the Hub does when a provider refuses an agent's turn because its usage allowance or plan quota is spent. The agent's queue is held until the stated reset, the refused input goes back uncounted, one operator turn may still pass, the queue is retried at the reset, job firings wait instead of failing, and the operator is told when the hold ends and how to get out of it.

Problem

A spent allowance refuses every turn until its reset, so without a hold the queue keeps starting turns that are refused the same way, burns its delivery attempts and gives up on input that would have succeeded after the reset, and jobs report failures that are only waiting.

Scope

In scope

  • Deriving a hold from the provider's most recent word about an agent, with its one-minute floor
  • Recognising a Copilot plan-quota refusal as an allowance refusal
  • Returning refused input uncounted and keeping the conversation's provider session
  • Letting one operator turn through a held queue
  • Retrying the queue at the hold's end, including across a Hub restart, and the operator events for each hold
  • Job firings that wait on a hold
  • The Copilot hold notice: its end time and the way out

Non-goals

  • Ordinary delivery: attempt counting, retries, give-up and refusal attribution (inbound-queue-delivery)
  • Token budgets and usage measurement (usage-accounting)
  • Binding an agent to another runner, and Copilot sign-in or version failures (runner-registry)
  • Job scheduling and firing records beyond the hold (agent-loops, loop-firing-accountability)

Requirements

FR-1# Requirement 1 of 7

SHALL The system SHALL hold an agent's queue, starting no turn for agent or job input, while the provider's most recent word about that agent (the newest turn outcome carrying an allowance reading or served tokens) is a refusal of its usage allowance, until the later of the stated reset time and one minute after the refusal; the hold SHALL be derived from recorded outcomes, not stored.

WhyEvery turn before the reset would be refused the same way; the one-minute floor stops a reset already in the past from becoming a cycle of refused turns, and deriving it lets a served turn end it.

FR-2# Requirement 2 of 7

SHALL The system SHALL treat a Copilot turn as refused by its usage allowance only when Copilot reports a structured error with `errorType` `quota` and `errorCode` `quota_exceeded` (as a session event or prompt error response), taking the reset from the run's own quota reading or else the project's newest Copilot reading whose reset is still ahead; with no reset known the refusal SHALL be recorded as an exhausted allowance and SHALL NOT hold the queue.

WhyThe quota belongs to the account, so another agent's reading states the same reset; recognising refusals from message wording, session caps, billing errors or rate limits would hold queues nobody decided to hold.

FR-3# Requirement 3 of 7

SHALL A run that failed because the provider refused the agent's usage allowance with a reset time later than the run's end SHALL return its input counting an allowance refusal instead of a failed delivery, and SHALL keep the conversation's provider session; a refusal whose reset is not later than the run's end SHALL count as a failed delivery.

WhyThe session and the input are both sound and the provider has said when it will serve them; a reset already past states no wait with an end, so the limits must still bound it.

FR-4# Requirement 4 of 7

SHALL While an agent's queue is held, operator input that arrived after the refusal SHALL let one turn start; if the provider refuses it too, the renewed hold SHALL stop input that arrived before it from starting another.

WhyOnly the operator can change the allowance (overage, credit, another runner), and a hold derived from the provider's last word cannot see that they did.

FR-5# Requirement 5 of 7

SHALL When a hold's end passes, the system SHALL attempt the agent's queue again without operator action, including a hold whose end passed while the Hub was stopped, and SHALL record an operator-visible event each time a refusal starts or renews a hold.

WhyNothing re-evaluates a queue on a timer, so the hold arms its own wake and startup re-arms every held queue.

FR-6# Requirement 6 of 7

SHALL A job firing whose input waits on a held queue, including the firing whose own turn was refused and one held across a Hub restart, SHALL be reported as in progress, not failed, and SHALL take the outcome of the turn that finally delivers it.

WhyIts input is waiting for a stated time, not refused.

FR-7# Requirement 7 of 7

SHALL While a Copilot quota refusal holds an agent's queue, the hold notice SHALL state the date and time it ends and the way out — bind the agent to another runner and then send it a message, since rebinding alone does not end the hold and its loops and jobs stay blocked — and the notices given to blocked loops and jobs SHALL state the date, not only the time.

WhyA Copilot plan quota resets monthly, so a time of day alone is ambiguous, and rebinding without a served turn changes nothing the hold can see.

Design

D1. Allowance holds are separate from usage-accounting: they govern the delivery queue, not measurement, and their trigger is a provider's word, not a Hub budget.

D2. Separate from inbound-queue-delivery because a hold suspends the attempt counting that capability defines: an allowance refusal with a reset ahead is counted apart and never trips the delivery limits.

D3. A hold is derived, not stored: it is read from the newest informative turn outcome (in inserted order), floored at one minute after the refusal, with a scheduled wake at its end that is re-armed at startup.
