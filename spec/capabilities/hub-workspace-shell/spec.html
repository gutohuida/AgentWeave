Hub workspace shell

capabilitycurrentsketch

20 MUST·1 SHOULD·21 requirements·47 criteria

Summary

The Hub's outer frame: a navigation rail beside a content area under a project header and project view tabs, drawn from one neutral graphite palette in light and dark modes, with hue reserved for meaning, a contrast-checked text ramp, opaque lifted surfaces, one place per project for configuration and agent creation, and a keyboard command palette.

Problem

Without one set of rules for planes, colour, emphasis and where navigation lives, each page invented its own chrome: competing fills and rules, accent-filled buttons, a rail that collapsed itself, configuration reachable from several places, and text levels that failed contrast.

Scope

In scope

  • Shell layout: rail, project header, project view tabs, content area
  • Palette, text ramp, accent and emphasis rules for the chrome
  • Rail collapse, width, and the navigation region's modes
  • Project configuration and agent creation entry points
  • The command palette

Non-goals

  • The agent and conversation tree inside the rail (agent-conversation-workspace)
  • Per-control hover, press and focus treatment (hub-interaction-feedback)
  • The composer's own controls (agent-composer)

Requirements

FR-1# Requirement 1 of 21

SHOULD The running shell SHOULD follow the approved mock (`openspec/changes/archive/2026-08-18-2026-07-30-hub-native-experience/mock-full.html`) for hierarchy, density, typography, radii and interaction states: a rail beside a project header, compact project view tabs (Overview, Tasks, Spec, Jobs, Activity) and bounded content, with no permanent global status strip. The mock's indigo and ink palette is superseded by the neutral graphite ramp, and AgentWeave keeps its own branding.

WhyThe mock remains the reference for structure; its hue was replaced on purpose.

FR-2# Requirement 2 of 21

MUST The rail and the content ground SHALL use adjacent steps of the same neutral ramp (`--rail` and `--bg`), so their roles differ by luminance alone, and their boundary MUST NOT combine a strong fill contrast with a strong dividing line.

WhyTwo related planes read as one application; a heavy line on top of a fill change reads as two.

FR-3# Requirement 3 of 21

MUST Every menu, popover, dialog, composer and other lifted surface SHALL take its background from a semantic token defined for both modes, and a modal dialog SHALL render an opaque panel over a separate scrim.

WhyAn undefined or transparent panel fill exposes the page beneath and makes text unreadable.

FR-4# Requirement 4 of 21

MUST Shell actions (add project, configure, create agent, expand, collapse, back) SHALL draw their icons from the application's single icon module (Lucide glyphs) and MUST NOT use literal geometric characters, icon-font text or corrupted glyphs; every icon-only control SHALL have an accessible name, and Add project and agent creation SHALL be available as readable text.

WhyTyped symbols rendered as mojibake on some systems; an unnamed icon button is unusable with assistive technology.

FR-5# Requirement 5 of 21

MUST A control that draws no border at rest MUST NOT gain one on hover; hover SHALL be expressed by fill, text or icon prominence. A control bordered at rest MAY change its border colour on hover, and keyboard focus indication is unaffected.

WhyAn outline appearing on hover reads as a state change the control does not have.

FR-6# Requirement 6 of 21

MUST The project header SHALL show the project's directory on its own line, below and separate from the agent count, as distinct path-segment elements (not one joined string), elided in the middle when too long so the leading and trailing segments stay visible, with the complete path available as the line's tooltip. Segments are not interactive and MUST NOT be styled as links. When the directory is unavailable the header SHALL say so instead.

WhyThe tail of a path is what identifies a project; a segment that navigates nowhere must not look clickable.

FR-7# Requirement 7 of 21

MUST The project view tabs SHALL sit on the ground plane with no fill of their own, and the boundary below them MUST NOT be drawn as a dividing rule.

WhyThe operator read a filled strip as a band laid over the screen; the active tab's own treatment marks position.

FR-8# Requirement 8 of 21

SHALL The rail SHALL change between expanded and collapsed only at the operator's request, and that choice SHALL persist across reloads. While collapsed it SHALL still reach every project and agent by accessible name, indicate the active ones, offer Add project, and offer the control that expands it again.

WhyA rail that renders no destinations is hidden, not collapsed; a page that needed width once took it from navigation and left no way back.

FR-20# Requirement 9 of 21

SHALL The expanded rail's width SHALL be adjustable by dragging its edge within fixed minimum and maximum widths and SHALL persist across reloads; the collapsed rail has one width and offers no drag handle.

WhyProject and conversation names vary in length; the operator decides how much room navigation gets.

FR-9# Requirement 10 of 21

MUST Every background, surface and border token of the chrome SHALL be neutral or near-neutral in both modes; hue SHALL be reserved for agent identity colours, semantic status colours and the single state accent. Components MUST NOT declare raw hex colours, except the light/dark preview swatches in setup and the sandboxed spec frame.

WhyA hex literal survives a palette swap unchanged and silently keeps the old palette alive.

FR-10# Requirement 11 of 21

SHALL The text ramp SHALL provide three distinguishable levels in both modes; every level and every status hue SHALL reach a contrast ratio of at least 3.0 on the ground plane and every elevated surface, the two primary levels SHALL reach 4.5, and an automated test against the stylesheet SHALL fail when any falls below its bar.

WhyRaising the third level to 4.5 collapses it into the second; 3.0 for incidental metadata and status hues is a recorded exemption, enforced rather than documented.

FR-11# Requirement 12 of 21

SHALL The primary control fill SHALL be monochrome (near-white on dark, near-black on light). Exactly one accent hue per mode SHALL be defined and used only for state such as the focus ring and selection, never as a control's fill.

WhyColour that fills controls competes with colour that carries meaning.

FR-12# Requirement 13 of 21

SHALL Every rail row on the open path (project, agent, conversation) SHALL expose `data-active="true"` and `aria-current`, and SHALL be graded by depth: the project row by weight only, the agent row by weight and full-strength text, and the open conversation row by weight, text and the only resting fill in navigation. No row SHALL carry a leading accent bar, and hover and press fills SHALL still apply over the selected state.

WhyThe conversation is the thing being read and the only row that needs to stand out; the leading bar was removed at the operator's request.

FR-13# Requirement 14 of 21

MUST The project header SHALL sit on the ground plane with no fill distinct from it and MUST NOT be closed by a dividing rule.

WhyA filled, ruled header reads as a box stacked on the page rather than part of it.

FR-21# Requirement 15 of 21

SHALL The project header SHALL offer a project switcher listing every registered project, a control that toggles light and dark mode, and a control that opens Hub setup.

WhySwitching project and mode are frequent and belong where the project is named.

FR-15# Requirement 16 of 21

MUST The application SHALL offer exactly two appearance choices, light and dark, and MUST NOT present a theme, palette or accent selector that does not change the rendered application.

WhyThe former five-theme picker offered choices that changed nothing.

FR-16# Requirement 17 of 21

MUST Navigation SHALL live in the rail only. Entering an area with its own navigation (a project's configuration sections, an agent's settings sections, the Spec screen's document tree) SHALL replace the rail's project tree with that area's navigation, identify the project, and offer one control that returns to the project tree. The content area MUST NOT render its own navigation column, and the rail's mode SHALL be derived from the current destination so a direct link, history navigation and an in-app control give the same result.

WhyTwo navigation columns compete for width and attention; deriving the mode from the URL keeps reloads and links consistent.

FR-17# Requirement 18 of 21

SHALL Each project's configuration SHALL be reachable from exactly one control, a Configure button on that project's rail row (shown on hover or focus, and always for the active project, and keyboard reachable); no other control in the workspace SHALL lead to that project's configuration.

WhyOne route that sits on the thing it configures is findable; several routes compete.

FR-18# Requirement 19 of 21

MUST The control that creates an agent SHALL be at the end of each project's agent list in the rail, and activating it SHALL navigate to that project before opening agent creation scoped to it; the project header MUST NOT offer agent creation.

WhyAgent creation reads the selected project, so opening it without navigating created the agent in the wrong project.

FR-22# Requirement 20 of 21

MUST The rail SHALL offer a single Add project action that opens a folder picker and registers the chosen folder, opening an existing project, adopting a marked directory, or initialising a plain folder; it MUST NOT offer separate open-existing and create-new actions.

WhyBoth entry points opened the same picker, and asking the operator to choose between them came when they knew least.

FR-19# Requirement 21 of 21

SHALL Ctrl+K or Cmd+K SHALL open, from anywhere in the Hub, a named dialog that searches the current project's conversations, agents, specification documents and tasks, showing each result's status and recency, and navigates to the selected one. Typing the key without its modifier in a text field MUST NOT open it, and dismissing it with Escape without a selection MUST NOT change what is displayed.

WhyThe operator should reach any thread, agent, document or task without walking the tree.

Design

D1. One neutral graphite ramp (`--bg`, `--rail`, `--surface`, `--surface-2`, `--surface-3`) per mode; hue only for agent identity, status and the `--ring` accent.

D2. Emphasis is monochrome: `--primary` is near-white on dark and near-black on light; the accent never fills a control.

D3. The third text level holds 3.0 rather than 4.5 so the ramp keeps three levels; `contrastRamp.test.ts` enforces every bar.

D4. Rail selection is a depth ladder (project, agent, conversation) with the conversation as the only filled row; the leading accent bar was removed at the operator's request and a test keeps it removed.

D5. Navigation lives in the rail; entering configuration, agent settings or the Spec screen swaps the rail's content, derived from the destination.

D6. Rail collapse and width are operator preferences in localStorage; no destination writes them.
