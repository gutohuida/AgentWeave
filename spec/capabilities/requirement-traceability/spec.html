Requirement traceability

capabilitycurrentsketch

42 MUST·42 requirements·88 criteria

Summary

The Hub indexes every requirement its specification documents declare, links tasks to the requirements they serve, records evidence pinned to the wording it demonstrates, decides that evidence through an append-only review, computes one coverage state per requirement, and watches the files each piece of evidence is about so that a later change raises a drift candidate for the operator to answer.

Problem

A requirement copied into a task as text cannot be joined, checked or noticed when it changes, and an agent's claim that it verified something is not a fact. Without an index, links, digest-pinned evidence and a single coverage definition, nobody can answer whether a requirement has work, whether that work was demonstrated against the current wording, whether the demonstration reached the product, or whether the implementation has since moved under it.

Scope

In scope

  • The requirement index, semantic digests, retirement and the digest change log
  • Task-to-requirement links and their resolution
  • Evidence recording, footprints, decisions and who may decide
  • Coverage states and integration answers
  • Drift detection and resolution
  • The coverage, evidence, drift, retired-requirement and task-card views

Non-goals

  • Task status transitions and who may approve a task (task-lifecycle-governance)
  • Merging an approved task's work into the main branch (task integration); only that a decision survives its failure is stated here
  • Document phases and the approval gate's other findings (spec-document-authority)
  • Storing evidence artifacts: the Hub records where an artifact is, not the artifact

Requirements

FR-7# Requirement 1 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL index every requirement a project's specification documents declare under the identifier the Hub minted for it, unique within its document, recording the declaring document, whether it is active or retired, its semantic digest with that digest's canonicalization version, and its anchor in the rendered document, and SHALL hold no requirement wording in the index.

WhyOther records point at a requirement without copying its wording. The index is derived from the documents and is not authoritative, so it must never hold text the documents do not.

FR-21# Requirement 2 of 42

SHALL The operator SHALL be able to rebuild the requirement index from the project's document files alone, yielding the same requirements, identifiers and digests; a document file that cannot be read SHALL leave its indexed requirements unchanged rather than retire them.

WhyA derived index must be reconstructible. Treating an unreadable file as an empty document would retire every requirement in it, and every link and piece of evidence would then point at retired rows.

FR-22# Requirement 3 of 42

SHALL Where a requirement is named by identifier alone and more than one document in the project declares that identifier, the Hub SHALL refuse the resolution as ambiguous (`identifier_ambiguous`) unless the document is also named.

WhyIdentifiers are minted per document, so `FR-3` names a different requirement in each. Choosing one would link work to a requirement nobody named, and the mistake would be invisible until someone read both documents.

FR-23# Requirement 4 of 42

SHALL A requirement's semantic digest SHALL be computed by one definition covering its modal, statement, party and acceptance criteria (independent of criteria order) and excluding its rationale, and SHALL be stored with the version of the canonicalization that produced it.

WhyThe digest marks what a reader must satisfy; explanatory prose changing must not make evidence stale. Recording the canonicalization keeps a digest taken under an older rule distinguishable from a rewording.

FR-24# Requirement 5 of 42

SHALL A requirement removed from its document SHALL be retired with its last digest rather than deleted, its identifier SHALL never be issued to another requirement, and a requirement restored to its document SHALL return under its old identifier.

WhyWork and evidence that pointed at a retired requirement must keep resolving: what it demanded, and what was built for it, outlive it.

FR-25# Requirement 6 of 42

SHALL Every change to an indexed requirement — created, reworded, retired or restored — SHALL be appended to a log recording the previous and new digest, whether the change came through the Hub or from an external edit, and the actor.

WhyStaleness and drift are judged against wording history; an append-only log is the only record of when and how a requirement changed.

FR-8# Requirement 7 of 42

SHALL A task SHALL be linked to the requirements it serves by reference to the indexed requirement, each link recording the actor that created it and, where an agent did, the run; links SHALL be added, never removed, including when the task reaches a terminal status.

WhyA copied string cannot be joined, checked, or notice a rewording; a link can. What work served a requirement is asked mostly about finished work, so links outlive the task's activity.

FR-26# Requirement 8 of 42

SHALL Requirement identifiers given when creating or updating a task SHALL be resolved all-or-nothing: a malformed, unknown or ambiguous identifier SHALL refuse the request with 422 (`identifier_malformed`, `identifier_unknown`, `identifier_ambiguous`) and create nothing, while the free-text `requirements` field SHALL never refuse and SHALL keep what does not resolve verbatim as an unresolved reference.

WhyA task that silently drops one of the requirements it was created for looks complete when it is not. Free text predates links and may hold prose; refusing it would break existing callers, and discarding it would lose what the author wrote.

FR-27# Requirement 9 of 42

SHALL Legacy free-text requirement references SHALL be converted into links where they resolve to a requirement in the same project — by migration and again whenever the operator reindexes — and those that do not resolve SHALL be preserved verbatim; no requirement SHALL be created to match one.

WhyOld tasks should join the traceability record without inventing requirements nobody wrote.

FR-12# Requirement 10 of 42

SHALL From a requirement it SHALL be possible to reach the tasks linked to it, the evidence recorded for it and its coverage; from a task it SHALL be possible to reach the requirements it serves with their current statements.

WhyTraceability is only useful if both questions — what serves this requirement, and what does this task promise — can be answered without a manual join.

FR-9# Requirement 11 of 42

SHALL Evidence for a requirement SHALL record its kind from an open set, a locator saying where its artifact is (not the artifact's content), a summary, its task, the actor and run taken from the caller's credential, and the requirement digest current when it was recorded.

WhyEvidence is whatever demonstrates the work, so kinds stay open. Pinning it to a digest is what makes staleness detectable. Identity comes from the credential because a body is caller-controlled. The database is not an artifact store.

FR-28# Requirement 12 of 42

SHALL Evidence recorded by an agent SHALL start awaiting review; evidence recorded by the operator SHALL be accepted on arrival with a review row saying so.

WhyAn agent's report that it verified something is a claim, not a fact; accepting it is the controlled act. The operator recording evidence is already that act.

FR-29# Requirement 13 of 42

SHALL Accepting or rejecting evidence SHALL be recorded as an append-only review attributed to the actor that decided; no decision is updated or deleted.

WhyWho judged what, and in what order, is the record; overwriting it would erase the history an approval rests on.

FR-30# Requirement 14 of 42

SHALL Evidence SHALL be decided only by the operator or by an agent the operator has granted the evidence-acceptance capability on that agent; a charter or anything an agent asserts SHALL NOT grant it, and where no agent holds it the operator decides.

WhyA charter describes behaviour, and behaviour is not authority. Operator-only acceptance is a supported way to work, not a degraded one.

FR-31# Requirement 15 of 42

SHALL An agent SHALL NOT decide evidence it recorded, judged on agent identity rather than run identity (`self_acceptance`).

WhyProducing evidence is open; accepting it is the gate. The same distinctness rule governs task approval.

FR-32# Requirement 16 of 42

SHALL A run delivered a task for review SHALL be refused when it records evidence for that task (`reviewer_records_evidence`), the refusal naming the task and telling it to move the task to `revision_needed` saying what is missing; a run working the task records evidence as before.

WhyA reviewer that records evidence and then approves it is judging its own work; one such row lifted a block the operator's rejection had placed.

FR-33# Requirement 17 of 42

SHALL Recording evidence for a retired requirement SHALL be refused (`requirement_retired`), and evidence recorded without a task SHALL take the task the recording run is bound to.

WhyA retired requirement asks for nothing new. Defaulting the task keeps an agent's evidence attached to the work it was doing.

FR-34# Requirement 18 of 42

SHALL Evidence matching an existing non-rejected piece on requirement, task, commit and actor SHALL be refused as `duplicate_evidence`, without telling an agent to commit its work; where the recording run's checkout is one the Hub commits at run end and holds uncommitted changes, a match with an earlier run's piece SHALL be recorded instead.

WhyDuplicates inflate coverage. An agent is told the Hub commits its work, so a refusal telling it to commit is wrong advice; and uncommitted work will be re-pointed to a new commit at run end, so it is not a duplicate.

FR-13# Requirement 19 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL refuse to accept or reject evidence while the run that recorded it is still executing, with a 409 (`recording_run_live`) that says it clears when that run ends and that stopping the run ends it, applying to the operator and granted agents alike and checked after the permanent refusals; a run marked running that the Hub is not executing SHALL NOT hold a decision, and the evidence view SHALL say whether its recording run is still executing.

WhyAn agent records evidence while its work is uncommitted, and the Hub re-points the evidence at the commit holding the work when the run ends; a decision before then judges a commit about to be replaced. It is a conflict that clears on its own, not a lack of permission.

FR-35# Requirement 20 of 42

SHALL Where a run records evidence for the same requirement and task again while its earlier piece is still undecided and the requirement's wording is unchanged, the Hub SHALL revise that piece in place — keeping its identity, taking the revision time as its recorded time, and saying it was a revision — rather than refuse or add a second piece.

WhyIt is the most recent recording of that demonstration, and whatever picks the most recent piece must pick it. A piece recorded against an earlier wording is not revised onto the current one.

FR-14# Requirement 21 of 42

SHALL When an agent's decision was refused because the recording run was executing, the Hub SHALL, once that run is no longer counted as executing, queue one Hub-origin note to that agent in the conversation where it was refused, naming each still-undecided piece and the commit it now names; no note SHALL be sent when every piece was decided meanwhile, when the conversation is closed or belongs to another agent, or for a refusal the operator received.

WhyThe refused agent's turn ended on the refusal; without the note a premature decision becomes a missing one. After a Hub restart the run is no longer executing and the decision is already open, so no note is owed.

FR-15# Requirement 22 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL answer an accept or reject with the decision it recorded even when integrating the task work that waited for that decision fails.

WhyAccepting evidence may merge an approved task's waiting work; a repository failure there is recorded as a skip. The decision stands, so a client told it failed would show a failure for a decision that was made.

FR-10# Requirement 23 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL compute a requirement's coverage state with a single definition that every surface reporting coverage uses.

WhyTwo implementations of "is this verified" eventually disagree, invisibly, until someone compares two screens.

FR-36# Requirement 24 of 42

SHALL Coverage SHALL take the first state that applies, in this order: `drifting` (an open drift candidate), `stale` (evidence only against an earlier digest), `evidence_awaiting_review`, `verified` (accepted current-digest evidence), `rejected` (all current-digest evidence rejected), `in_progress` (linked work started or completed without evidence), `not_started`, `unserved` (no linked work).

WhyThe order ranks how much the evidence can be trusted, worst news first, so a reworded or drifting requirement is never reported verified. `rejected` is distinct so a refused demonstration is not mistaken for work not yet evidenced.

FR-37# Requirement 25 of 42

SHALL A requirement with no identifier or no digest SHALL be reported as a diagnostic outside coverage; a retired requirement with no linked work SHALL be reported as `retired`, never `unserved`; and a document SHALL be able to list its active requirements that have no linked work.

WhyA broken requirement is not unserved, it is broken. `unserved` means somebody should build it, which a retired requirement does not ask for.

FR-38# Requirement 26 of 42

SHALL Every coverage entry the Hub returns SHALL carry an integration answer — `integrated`, `not_integrated`, `unknown` (no main branch) or `not_applicable` (no accepted current-digest evidence) — saying whether the accepted evidence's footprint is reachable from the main branch, and integration SHALL NOT be a coverage state.

WhyAccepted evidence can describe code that never reached the product; reporting `verified` alone would be true of the branch and false of the product. Integration is independent of evidence quality, so ranking them together would force a choice between "stale but merged" and "verified but unmerged".

FR-39# Requirement 27 of 42

SHALL Recording evidence SHALL capture an implementation footprint: in a git repository, a commit and the blob identifiers of the files it watches; outside one, those paths and a content hash of each.

WhyA project without a repository is a supported first-class case and would otherwise be permanently unverifiable.

FR-40# Requirement 28 of 42

SHALL An agent's footprint SHALL be taken from its own working checkout (or, once a task's checkout is released, the task branch tip) whatever commit its locator names, and SHALL be re-stamped at the commit holding its work when the run ends.

WhyThe agent's checkout is the work in progress; a later re-stamp corrects it once the Hub commits it. Honouring a commit an agent names would let it describe a tree other than the one it worked in.

FR-41# Requirement 29 of 42

SHALL An operator's footprint SHALL be taken from the commit they name — the `commit` field, else a locator that is a bare git object name — and otherwise from their own checkout; a named commit the repository does not have SHALL be refused (`locator_commit_unknown`) with no evidence recorded.

WhyReview checks out the footprinted commit and integration merges on its reachability, so a footprint silently describing a tree other than the one named is worse than none. A locator is otherwise a path; reading paths as revisions would be a guess.

FR-42# Requirement 30 of 42

SHALL The response to recording evidence SHALL report the captured footprint, including where its watched files came from and how many there are, and an agent's recording response SHALL mark it provisional.

WhyThe recorder sees which tree the evidence was attached to while it can still correct it; an agent's footprint will be re-stamped at run end.

FR-43# Requirement 31 of 42

SHALL A footprint SHALL report where the run that produced it wrote outside the directory it was taken from, SHALL still describe that directory, and the evidence SHALL NOT be refused for it.

WhyA run's workspace is a working directory, not a wall, so a footprint can miss part of the work. Moving it to another tree would be a guess (one may be the operator's unrelated checkout); refusing would turn an observation into a gate.

FR-19# Requirement 32 of 42

SHALL A footprint SHALL watch only the files its evidence is about, taken from the first of these sources that names any file: the paths its locator names that exist in the tree, the files changed by the commit an operator names, and the files changed on its line of work since it left the main line; a footprint naming none SHALL watch nothing and say why, and recording SHALL NOT be refused for it.

WhySources are tried in order, not combined: combined, every agent row watched its task's whole diff, and one commit to a shared file raised candidates for about half the accepted evidence. A locator is read as words, and only words that are paths in the tree count.

FR-44# Requirement 33 of 42

SHALL A drift scan SHALL rebuild footprints recorded before footprints recorded what they watch — from the locator at the verified commit, keeping that commit as baseline, or else from the files their merge into the main line brought in, taking the main line at rebuild time as baseline — and one that cannot be rebuilt SHALL be listed as `recorded_before_watching`.

WhyOld evidence should join drift detection without its first scan reporting every file anyone has touched since the merge.

FR-11# Requirement 34 of 42

SHALL A drift scan SHALL raise a candidate for accepted, current-digest evidence whose watched files changed on its comparison line — its own line of work until that reaches the main branch, the main branch after — SHALL raise nothing where that comparison cannot be made, SHALL NOT scan a footprint that watches nothing, and SHALL NOT edit any specification document.

WhyThat an implementation changed is observable; that a requirement should change is a judgement, and inferring it would rewrite an approved specification on a file diff. Overlap is a candidate signal, not proof, so an unknown comparison raises nothing rather than a guess.

FR-45# Requirement 35 of 42

SHALL Only the operator SHALL resolve a drift candidate, as specification updated, implementation corrected, or no specification change required, recording the digest current at that moment; the first and third answers SHALL also record the current fingerprint so the same change is not raised again, and `implementation_corrected` SHALL NOT.

Why"Implementation corrected" claims the change was undone; silencing it would turn a mistaken answer into a permanent blind spot, so the same change still present is drift again.

FR-46# Requirement 36 of 42

SHALL Rewording or retiring a requirement SHALL mark its open drift candidates `superseded`.

WhyA candidate asks whether the requirement or the implementation is wrong; once the requirement changed, that question is about a wording that no longer exists.

FR-20# Requirement 37 of 42

SHALL The operator's view of a specification document SHALL offer a project-wide drift scan, show that document's open candidates with the requirement's identifier, the files that moved and the evidence that had been verified, and let the operator answer each there; a second answer to a candidate SHALL be refused (409 `drift_not_open`) naming the answer it has, and scanning and answering SHALL be announced to every open view of the project.

WhyA state whose remedy cannot be performed where it is shown is a defect. Raising and answering ship together: a scan never raises the same question twice, so a scan without answers could leave a gated requirement permanently unapprovable.

FR-47# Requirement 38 of 42

SHALL The approval gate's refusal for a drifting requirement SHALL say that the operator answers the drift candidate on the document and that an agent cannot.

WhyThe refusal is where the operator and agents learn what unblocks approval.

FR-16# Requirement 39 of 42

SHALL Where the operator's coverage view reports that a requirement's evidence awaits a decision, it SHALL list each piece in the order the Hub returns them — with summary, recorder, locator, commit and branch, task, state and latest decision reason, the most recent marked without claiming it is the one a merge takes — and SHALL let the operator accept it, or reject it with a reason; where accepting can merge the piece's commit, the piece SHALL say so beside Accept, and a Hub refusal SHALL be shown beside the piece in the Hub's words.

WhyA screen that says a judgement is the operator's must let them make it; accepted evidence is what approval merges, so a decision that can change the main branch must not look like a label.

FR-48# Requirement 40 of 42

SHALL A decision on evidence, and the end of a run that recorded evidence (after it stops counting as live), SHALL be announced to every open view of the project, so coverage and the task board update without a reload and a piece that was still being recorded becomes decidable.

WhySeveral screens show the same state; one refreshing while another shows the old state is how an operator acts on stale information.

FR-17# Requirement 41 of 42

SHALL The task card SHALL show a chip for each requirement the task serves, toned by that requirement's coverage on its document (verified, awaiting review, rejected, otherwise neutral), opening the requirement in its document when one resolves, and SHALL add a count by state when it serves more than four requirements.

WhyA task may serve a whole slice; a card showing only identifiers reads as one unit and hides a rejected requirement inside it. Using the document's coverage means the card cannot disagree with the document view.

FR-18# Requirement 42 of 42

SHALL The operator's view of a document SHALL list the requirements it has retired by identifier and key, and from each SHALL reach the tasks and evidence still linked to it and its coverage.

WhyRetired requirements are kept so their links and evidence survive; coverage excludes them, so without this list they leave every screen while work still points at them.

Behaviour

Coverage state of one requirement

  1. If it has no identifier or no digest, report a diagnostic and no state.
  2. If an open drift candidate exists: drifting.
  3. Else if its evidence is only against an earlier digest: stale.
  4. Else if current-digest evidence awaits review: evidence_awaiting_review.
  5. Else if current-digest evidence is accepted: verified.
  6. Else if current-digest evidence exists and all of it is rejected: rejected.
  7. Else if linked work is started or completed: in_progress; if linked work exists: not_started.
  8. Else: retired when the requirement is retired, otherwise unserved.
  9. Independently, answer integration from the accepted current-digest evidence's footprints: integrated, not_integrated, unknown (no main branch) or not_applicable.

Files a footprint watches

  1. Read the locator as words; strip `::node` and `:line` suffixes; keep the words that are paths, or directories holding paths, in the footprinted tree.
  2. If none, and an operator named a commit, take the files that commit changed (a merge against its first parent).
  3. If none, take the files changed on the line of work since it left the main line.
  4. If none, watch nothing with reason `names_no_file`.

Design

D1 — Derived index, document-scoped identity. The index is rebuilt from the files and holds no wording; identifiers are per document, so records point at the indexed row, and a bare identifier shared by two documents is refused rather than guessed.

D2 — Producing evidence is open; accepting it is the gate. Anyone working a task may record evidence; agents' evidence waits for review; decisions are append-only, per-agent granted by the operator, never self-decided, and never by the task's reviewer recording its own.

D3 — Evidence is pinned twice: to the requirement digest (so rewording makes it stale) and to an implementation footprint (so a code change can raise drift and integration can be judged).

D4 — Footprints describe the tree that was named or worked in, never a guessed one: an agent's own checkout re-stamped at run end, an operator's named commit or checkout. Writes outside the workspace are reported, not chased or refused.

D5 — Watch narrowly. Watched files come from the first source that names any (locator, named commit, branch diff), never their union, so one shared-file commit does not flood candidates.

D6 — Drift is a question for the operator, never an edit. Three answers; `implementation_corrected` keeps no fingerprint so a recurrence is raised again; rewording supersedes open candidates.

D7 — One coverage computation, with integration reported beside it rather than ranked within it.

D8 — Decisions wait for the recording run to end, because the Hub re-points that run's evidence at its commit then; the held agent is told when it can decide.
