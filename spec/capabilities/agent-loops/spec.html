Agent loops

capabilitycurrentsketch

41 MUST·1 SHOULD·42 requirements·138 criteria

Summary

A loop is a scheduled job given a purpose and a stop condition. Each firing claims work from the loop's queue of tasks, briefs the loop's agent with a bounded handover from the previous firing, and records what it did; the loop ends when its stop condition is met or the operator stops it, and is archived rather than deleted.

Problem

Unattended recurring work needs to know what it is for, what to work on next, when to stop, and to leave an account of every firing that an operator can trust, without piling up stale work or misleading records while the agent is busy or the queue is stuck.

Scope

In scope

  • Opting a job into a loop; loop fields, the declared document and the evidence declaration
  • The loop's queue: membership, authorship, adoption, control
  • What a firing does: busy guard, stop condition, claim, stall, in-flight, briefing
  • Staged edits, stopping, archiving, re-enabling
  • Loop and job routes and the loop's surfaces in the app

Non-goals

  • Reviewer resolution and multi-agent staffing of flows (agent-flows)
  • Task transitions and integration on approval (task-lifecycle-governance)
  • Binding runs to tasks (run-task-binding)

Requirements

FR-26# Requirement 1 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL make a scheduled job a loop when a purpose, a stop time, or the stop-when-the-queue-empties condition is supplied for it, at creation or by a later update, and SHALL refuse any other loop field (a specification document, the evidence declaration, a stop reason) on a job that is not a loop and is not becoming one, naming what would make it one.

WhyOpting in changes nothing about the job's cron, message, agent or history; a job never opted in behaves as a plain scheduled job. A loop field silently dropped would leave the caller believing the job does something it does not.

FR-31# Requirement 2 of 42

SHALL A loop MAY declare one specification document as the source of its queue, given by the document's id or by its `spec/` path; the Hub SHALL store the document's id, SHALL refuse a path that names no document, and SHALL refuse a declaration of a document that another unarchived loop already declares, naming that loop.

WhyOne live loop per document keeps ownership of the document's tasks unambiguous. A loop that has ended but is not archived still holds its document: archiving is the operator's statement that it is finished with it.

FR-27# Requirement 3 of 42

SHALL A loop's queue SHALL be exactly the tasks whose `loop_id` names it. `loop_id` SHALL be set only when a task is created (by the approval of a document the loop declares, or by a caller permitted to add to the loop's queue) or when a loop adopts a document's tasks; an update supplying `loop_id` SHALL be refused, and the task list scoped to a loop SHALL return every task naming it, whatever its status.

WhyA loop's stop-when-empty condition and its history both read the queue, so its membership must not change behind them.

FR-32# Requirement 4 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL accept a task created with a `loop_id` only from the operator or from a run of the agent the loop's job names, and SHALL refuse any other caller with 403, naming `send_message` to that agent as the way to ask for the addition.

WhyThe agent a loop's job names is treated as the loop's creator. An agent whose roster entry is archived loses the privilege. Tasks a document's approval creates are not subject to this rule.

FR-38# Requirement 5 of 42

SHALL Each loop SHALL have a controller that decides whether the agent its job names may add tasks after the loop's first firing: the operator by default, or that agent once the operator delegates control. While the operator controls, that agent's direct additions after the first firing SHALL be refused, naming `ask_user` as the way to ask the operator.

WhyBefore the first firing the agent is still defining its queue and is let through. Delegation and taking back are operator-only and are recorded against the loop with actor and time. A delegation is to one agent: when a change of the job's agent is applied, control returns to the operator and that return is recorded.

FR-40# Requirement 6 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL refuse every caller, the operator included, a task added to a loop that has ended, stating how and when it ended and echoing the submitted task so that it can be resubmitted as initial work of a new loop. The refusal SHALL NOT restart the loop.

WhyAn ended loop's queue is closed; reviving it would contradict its recorded ending.

FR-34# Requirement 7 of 42

SHALL Each firing of a loop SHALL be briefed with the most recent checkpoint of a prior firing of that loop (for a review turn, the checkpoint of the task's author where one exists), and a loop's job SHALL refuse `session_mode` `resume`, at creation or by update, naming checkpoint continuity.

WhyEvery firing starts a new conversation, so the checkpoint is what carries one firing's outcome into the next.

FR-35# Requirement 8 of 42

SHALL A loop firing's briefing SHALL carry the loop's purpose, the task claimed for it, and the prior checkpoint, the checkpoint truncated to a fixed bound (4,000 characters) when longer.

WhyA long-running loop's accumulated history must not grow what one firing is asked to read.

FR-61# Requirement 9 of 42

SHALL A loop MAY declare at creation whether its work needs evidence before it is integrated; the declaration SHALL be stored as made or not made, SHALL be refused on any later update, and where none was made the Hub SHALL resolve it when asked: evidence governs a loop that declares a document and any task linked to a requirement, and does not govern a task of a documentless loop that has no requirement link.

WhyThe declaration decides what approving a task writes into the operator's repository, so it cannot change part-way through a queue. Storing a copy of today's default would keep asserting it after the default moved. A declaration actually made wins over the default in both directions.

FR-30# Requirement 10 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL evaluate a loop's stop condition only within a firing its cron or a manual trigger already caused, and when it is met SHALL skip that firing, record the reason, end the loop and disable and unschedule its job. Stop-when-empty SHALL be met only when the queue has held tasks and none is still open.

WhyThe stop condition never creates a firing or decides what an agent does. A queue that was never filled is not drained, so a loop created before its work exists keeps running.

FR-36# Requirement 11 of 42

SHALL When a loop ends because its queue drained, the Hub SHALL record a `loop_queue_exhausted` event naming any outstanding request at that moment: an unanswered question in the loop's most recent earlier firing conversation, or else a message from the loop's agent to the agent whose run created the loop that is still queued or was delivered into a turn still running. The loop SHALL stop either way.

WhyAn outstanding request does not create a waiting state, but whoever reviews the stop should see it. Outstanding is read from the message's delivery, not from a read flag nothing sets.

FR-53# Requirement 12 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL refuse a loop's firing, before it records, claims or queues anything, when the agent its job names is running a turn or is held by a provider usage-allowance refusal, and in addition the loop's queue has no open task, or the loop declares no document, or no other agent in the project is free. The job SHALL stay enabled and scheduled.

WhyA briefing queued for a busy or held agent is stale before it is read, and one per tick accumulates. A documentless loop staffs only its own agent, so another free agent cannot lift the refusal; a flow can staff a free agent for open work. Where this refusal would refuse the next firing, the loop's summary reports its reason rather than a stall.

FR-33# Requirement 13 of 42

SHALL A loop firing that is not refused SHALL select its work deterministically, resuming tasks already active before claiming the oldest startable entry-status task, and SHALL record each selected task as claimed (`pending` to `assigned`, assignee set) before the agent's turn begins. A refused firing SHALL change no task.

WhyThe choice of task is never left to the agent. Under a flow the same decision also chooses the agent for each task.

FR-51# Requirement 14 of 42

SHALL A firing SHALL claim only a task the dependency gate would let start, using the gate's own determination, and SHALL skip unstartable tasks in queue order to claim the oldest startable one.

WhyIf claimability and startability disagree, the loop claims work it cannot begin.

FR-54# Requirement 15 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL skip a firing whose queue holds open tasks of which none is claimable, before queueing any input, recording the most specific reason it established: a named task the walk could not staff, else the dependency gating, else the count of open tasks by status. The job SHALL stay enabled and scheduled.

WhyA stalled queue is not a finished one, and the operator resolving the stall is enough for a later firing to proceed. The named task is what the operator can act on; a status histogram is the reason of last resort.

FR-52# Requirement 16 of 42

SHALL A loop whose queue is gated only by unmet dependencies SHALL be treated as stalled, never stopped, and its stall reason SHALL distinguish tasks awaiting a prerequisite's approval from tasks gated on a rejected prerequisite.

WhyA rejection is reversible by the operator; stopping would disable and unschedule the job so that reversing the rejection could not revive it. The two causes have different remedies.

FR-69# Requirement 17 of 42

SHALL In a loop that declares no specification document the Hub SHALL staff a task nobody holds only with the agent the loop's job names, never substituting another agent, and SHALL NOT replace an existing assignee. A loop that declares a document MAY staff any available agent.

WhyA documentless loop is one agent's loop. The one exception is reviewer recovery: replacing an author wrongly holding a review is not narrowed to the job's agent, since that agent is the author. The restriction filters agents already judged available and adds no availability condition of its own.

FR-62# Requirement 18 of 42

SHALL A loop that declares no document SHALL NOT select one of its completed tasks for review and SHALL NOT report such a task as a step it could not staff; where it has nothing to claim and holds completed tasks, its stall reason SHALL say that finished work is waiting for the operator to land it.

WhyA loop has one agent, and every review it could staff would name the author, which author and reviewer separation refuses. The operator may still dispatch a review by hand, and a review already held by its author is still recovered by reassignment without moving status.

FR-39# Requirement 19 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL accept an edit to a loop's purpose, stop condition or default agent at any time, stage it, report it separately from the definition in force, and apply it at the start of the loop's next firing, recording both the staging and the application against the loop with actor and time. A firing already running keeps the definition it was briefed with.

WhyWhere none of the loop's firings is running, the staged edit is applied before the busy guard, so the guard and the summary ask about the agent that will run. A staged agent archived meanwhile is dropped and the application records why; the rest of the edit applies. Tasks and input already assigned or queued for the old agent stay with it.

FR-71# Requirement 20 of 42

SHALL An operator's stop of a loop, stated as a stop reason or made by archiving its job, SHALL end the loop as `stopped` (never `completed`, whatever the reason text), disable and unschedule its job, and record a `loop_stopped` event in the same transaction, broadcast to listening clients. A stop of a loop that has already ended SHALL be refused with 409, leaving its recorded ending unchanged.

WhyAn ending is written once. Control changes and archiving are likewise recorded in the same transaction as the change, so neither exists without the other.

FR-43# Requirement 21 of 42

MUST The Hub MUST refuse to delete a job or a loop, naming archiving instead, and SHALL offer archiving, which hides them from default listings and keeps their purpose, queue, stop state, event history and run records. Archiving a loop SHALL be operator-only, and an agent SHALL NOT archive a job that has a loop.

WhyUnattended work must stay accountable after it ends. A job's run history is bounded: the Hub keeps the most recent 100 run records per job.

FR-44# Requirement 22 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL refuse to archive a loop that has not ended, and SHALL end and archive a loop together with its job when the operator archives the job.

WhyArchiving must never hide work that is still firing; archiving the job is the operator's way to retire a running loop in one step, since an archived job never fires.

FR-45# Requirement 23 of 42

SHALL A loop that ends SHALL record how it ended as a value, `completed` when its queue drained and `stopped` otherwise, separately from its written reason.

WhyCompletion must be countable without interpreting prose.

FR-72# Requirement 24 of 42

SHALL The app SHALL let the operator edit a loop's name, default agent, message, schedule, purpose and stop condition from the loop's own view, sending only the changed fields; name, message and schedule apply at once, purpose, stop condition and default agent at the next firing.

WhyThe declared document and the evidence declaration are shown but not editable; an ended or archived loop is read-only; a stop time can be replaced but not cleared; a flow cannot be saved without a stop condition; the view says a new default agent takes only work nobody holds.

FR-70# Requirement 25 of 42

SHALL A loop's own view SHALL offer the operator Stop while it has not ended, Archive once it has ended and is not archived, and delegate or take back queue control while it has not ended, SHALL say who currently decides additions, and SHALL show any refusal in the Hub's own text.

WhyOffering an action only where the Hub would accept it keeps the view honest; a stop does not interrupt a running firing, and the confirmation says so.

FR-73# Requirement 26 of 42

SHALL When the agent a job names changes, the Hub SHALL clear the provider session the job would resume; for a plain job at once, for a loop when the change is applied at its next firing.

WhyA session belongs to the agent that held it.

FR-74# Requirement 27 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL refuse with 403 a change to the agent a job names when it comes from an agent's run, saying only the operator can make it, before anything is applied or staged.

WhyA job's agent decides who works it and, under delegated control, who may add to a loop's queue.

FR-57# Requirement 28 of 42

SHALL Creating or updating a job SHALL be refused with 400 when it names an archived agent (saying it is archived) or, where the project's roster is known, an agent not on it (saying it is not one of the project's agents and listing the roster).

WhyA typo otherwise produces a job that fails every tick. A project with no known agents is left alone, because creating a job before any agent is known is an ordinary order. An agent that exists but has no runner bound is reported at firing time as having no runner.

FR-29# Requirement 29 of 42

SHALL For a loop, the job listing, a job's detail and the loop routes SHALL report its label, agent, declared document, purpose, stop condition, evidence declaration, ending state, stop reason and time, queue counts by status, current items, stall reason, controller, pending edit, whether a firing is active, and the count of questions from its firings that are unanswered, not declined and still being waited on. A plain job SHALL carry none of this.

WhyA question whose wait ended unanswered is not waiting on anyone and is not counted.

FR-46# Requirement 30 of 42

MUST A loop's current items SHALL be every task the next firing would select plus any blocked task, each naming its agent and that agent's capacity; a task MUST NOT be omitted because the claim moved it to `assigned`.

WhyCurrent items are a set because a flow may select several tasks; a blocked task cannot be claimed but is still the loop's work and is what the operator must see.

FR-47# Requirement 31 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL list a project's loops without any conversation id, excluding archived loops unless asked to include them, and SHALL return any single loop by id, archived or not, with its recent firings and its recent events.

WhyEach firing occupies its own conversation, so no conversation is the loop.

FR-41# Requirement 32 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL record against a loop, each with its actor and time, every staged and applied edit, change of control, stop, archiving, task adoption and stalled firing, and SHALL return those events scoped to that loop alone; the loop's firings SHALL be retrievable from its job's run history.

WhyA loop's story should be answerable without filtering a project-wide log by hand.

FR-48# Requirement 33 of 42

SHALL Where conversations are listed, a conversation created by a loop firing SHALL name that loop by its label and lead to the loop's own view; a conversation created by a plain job or by the operator SHALL name no loop.

WhyFirings start conversations the operator never began; naming them lets the operator tell them apart without a second copy of the loop's history.

FR-50# Requirement 34 of 42

SHALL Where conversations are listed, consecutive conversations created by the same loop for the same agent SHALL collapse into one expandable row naming the loop and the count, without reordering the list; the row SHALL say when a firing in it is waiting for the operator, and SHALL open when it holds the conversation being read.

WhyA change of agent breaks the run, because under a flow the hand-over is the informative event.

FR-75# Requirement 35 of 42

SHALL The Hub SHALL refuse with 409 (code `loop_ended`) re-enabling the job of a loop that has ended, naming how and when it ended and that a new loop is the remedy; re-enabling a job merely paused SHALL still be accepted.

WhyAn ended loop's queue is closed, so a re-enabled job would end again at its next firing.

FR-76# Requirement 36 of 42

SHALL An archived job SHALL never fire again: enabling it or running it by hand SHALL be refused with 409 (code `job_archived`), and the scheduler SHALL NOT register it at start; running a disabled job by hand SHALL be refused with 400.

WhyThere is no unarchive; the remedy is a new job.

FR-77# Requirement 37 of 42

SHALL Creating or updating a job SHALL refuse with 400 a cron expression that is malformed or that restricts both day-of-month and day-of-week.

WhyAn expression restricting both day fields fires on a different date than the one the Hub stores and shows.

FR-78# Requirement 38 of 42

SHALL A loop MAY be created with initial tasks; every entry SHALL be checked before anything is created, so that one refused entry creates no job, loop or task, and an agent SHALL be refused seeding a loop whose job names a different agent.

WhyA loop half-created behind a refused task would be enabled with a queue nobody intended.

FR-79# Requirement 39 of 42

SHOULD Creating a loop SHOULD return a continuity warning when the project has checkpointing off or no checkpoint runner, saying each firing will start with no memory of the last.

WhyA loop with no memory is legitimate but should not be a surprise; creation is when it can still be acted on. The warning never refuses.

FR-80# Requirement 40 of 42

SHALL An agent run SHALL be able to create, update, run or archive jobs only when the project allows agent jobs and the run is live and correctly attributed; archiving a plain job from an agent SHALL additionally require the operator's direction for that archive.

WhyRecurring work is a standing commitment of the operator's resources; the allowance is a project setting, and archiving is a decision the operator makes per job.

FR-81# Requirement 41 of 42

SHALL Every firing's briefing SHALL lead with whether the agent works inside a flow or a loop and SHALL tell it to finish the given task and stop; only a flow's briefing SHALL state that finished work is offered for review by another agent.

WhyAn agent in a flow did not choose to be there; routing is the flow's job. Telling a loop's agent that someone reviews its work would be false. The statement precedes the prior checkpoint so truncation of a long checkpoint cannot remove it.

FR-82# Requirement 42 of 42

SHALL Every firing that claims a task for work SHALL name in its briefing the `update_task` calls that finish it, legal from the task's current status, and what a turn that ends with the task unmoved costs. Where the task serves requirements of record the briefing SHALL name them and `record_evidence`; where it serves none it SHALL say nothing about evidence. A flow's briefing SHALL say that notes for the reviewer are delivered only when the task reaches `completed`. For a task returned for revision it SHALL say who sent it back and quote their notes, and, measured when composed, whether the task's branch still merges into the main branch, naming conflicting paths and the remedy; a failed measurement SHALL NOT withhold the briefing.

WhyMeasured: agents call the tool the briefing names, not one listed only in the tool inventory. An instruction to record evidence against a requirement that does not exist is refused when followed. A rework brief that carried only the description sent an author back over a merge conflict to complete the same commit again.

Design

D1 A loop is a job with a Loop row. A job opts in by a purpose or a stop condition; the loop keeps the job's cron, message and agent. A loop that declares a specification document is a flow (agent-flows); a documentless loop staffs only its job's agent.

D2 One decision per firing. `decide_firing` (scheduler.py) is the single walk that chooses tasks and agents, decides stall or in-flight, and feeds both the firing and the loop summary, so the board and the firing cannot disagree.

D3 Order inside a firing: apply a staged edit if no firing of the loop is running; busy guard (records nothing); for a plain job, held-queue coalescing; stop condition (ends the loop); decision (in flight records nothing; stall records one row, then ticks); claim and queue; the conversation is created only once the firing proceeds.

D4 Endings are written once (`loop_ending.end_loop`): reason, time, `completed`/`stopped`, and the job disabled, in one transaction with the `loop_stopped` event. Ended loops refuse re-enabling and new tasks; nothing deletes a job or loop.

D5 Queue authorship: the job's agent stands for the loop's creator. Before the first firing it may add freely; after, only with delegated control; the operator always may; an ended loop refuses everyone. `loop_id` is write-once at creation, plus adoption at a document claim.

D6 Edits to purpose, stop condition and default agent are staged and applied at the next firing; name, message and schedule apply at once. The evidence declaration is fixed at creation because it is read at approval, not by a firing.

D7 Run history is bounded (most recent 100 records per job) and every stored reason fits 500 characters; refusals that carry no new fact write no row.
