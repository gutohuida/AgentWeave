Spec document authority

capabilitycurrentsketch

95 MUST·2 SHOULD·1 MAY·98 requirements·277 criteria

Summary

Where a specification document lives, who may write it, how its requirements are identified, what phase and rigor it is in, and who may move it. The document is a file in the project; an agent submits a structured payload and the Hub renders it, so the format is a schema rather than a request. Phase and rigor are held by the Hub and changed only by the operator. A finished change reaches the capability corpus through an audited merge (a fold), and a document can be archived or, if it never shipped, deleted.

Problem

A specification is only authoritative if nobody can forge its approval, quietly rewrite what was approved, or change the record of current behaviour without saying where the change came from. An agent can write any file in its checkout, so every one of those guarantees has to be held by the Hub, not asked of the author.

Scope

In scope

  • The document file, its structured payload, requirement identity and digests
  • Phase and its transitions, completeness checks, approval and archive authority
  • Rigor and the proposals a contract or gate document records instead of applying edits
  • Naming, renaming, reading and rendering a document
  • Writing capability documents through merges and folds; deleting a document

Non-goals

  • Creating tasks and flows at approval, and refreshing them on re-approval (task-lifecycle-governance, agent-flows)
  • Evidence decisions, coverage computation and drift review screens (requirement-traceability)
  • The specification chat surface (spec-chat-session) and roadmaps and their slices (spec-roadmaps)

Requirements

FR-40# Requirement 1 of 98

SHALL Specification documents SHALL be files beneath the project's working directory, read from disk on every request, with every path resolved through the project workspace, which refuses absolute paths, traversal segments, control characters and symlink escapes.

WhyThe file is the document. The Hub keeps no second copy of its content and has no route by which a client pushes one, so there is nothing to reconcile.

FR-41# Requirement 2 of 98

SHALL An agent SHALL author a specification only by submitting a structured payload; the Hub SHALL validate it, render the document's markup itself and write it, and SHALL refuse an invalid payload with an error naming the offending field, writing nothing.

WhyA schema the agent cannot violate replaces a markup contract it is merely asked to honour.

FR-42# Requirement 3 of 98

SHALL Every payload SHALL declare `schema_version`; a missing or unsupported version SHALL be refused by name, and a field a part does not define SHALL be kept unchanged across a read and a rewrite of the document.

WhyA rewrite must lose nothing the current schema cannot name. Keeping a field is not reading it: see the unknown_field finding.

FR-121# Requirement 4 of 98

SHALL A payload in the shape `read_spec_document` returns SHALL be accepted as a submission without loss: criteria nested under a requirement join the top-level list under that requirement's key, the read-only `identifier`, `state` and `anchor` fields are dropped and a retired requirement is left out; a requirement from a truncated read, or a nested criterion that conflicts with a top-level one, SHALL be refused.

WhyAgents edit by reading a document and resubmitting it. Resubmitted as read, the nested criteria were kept as unknown fields and the top-level list was empty, so every criterion was deleted (F452).

FR-43# Requirement 5 of 98

MUST The Hub SHALL assign each requirement its identifier (`FR-<n>`), correlated across submissions by the requirement's key; an identifier SHALL stay with its key whatever the text, position or schema version, SHALL never be reused after its requirement is removed, and an identifier or identity block supplied in a submission SHALL be ignored.

WhyTasks and evidence point at identifiers. Correlating by position would renumber every requirement after an insertion and silently re-target what points at them.

FR-48# Requirement 6 of 98

SHALL The Hub SHALL store a content digest for every document it writes and a digest for each requirement's text, and SHALL never resolve a file that differs from its recorded digest on its own initiative: a write submitted over a diverged file replaces it and reports the divergence in its answer.

WhyChoosing between a hand edit and the recorded version is the operator's decision; the Hub surfaces both digests and waits.

FR-96# Requirement 7 of 98

SHALL Serving a document whose file no longer matches the digest the Hub recorded SHALL mark it as diverged, with both digests, in the operator's document read, in the document listing and in an agent's read, without refusing the read.

WhyThe phase is read from a row, but the content is served from the file; unmarked, approval would attach to a path rather than to the bytes anyone reads. Editing on the way to a new revision is legitimate, so the read is marked, not refused.

FR-47# Requirement 8 of 98

SHALL The Hub SHALL append an event for every change to a document's content, phase, path, rendering or merge record, naming the actor, its origin (operator control, agent submission, or Hub lifecycle) and the run where there is one; events SHALL never be edited or deleted.

WhyThe history is the record of who did what to a document; an editable record is not one.

FR-80# Requirement 9 of 98

SHALL Every content write SHALL record its actor as established from the caller's credential (the operator for a project credential, the agent and its run for a run credential) and never from anything in the request body.

WhyAn operator's write must be distinguishable from an agent's, so a corpus written by hand is never mistaken for one an agent authored, and an actor a caller can state is an actor a caller can invent.

FR-44# Requirement 10 of 98

SHALL A document's phase SHALL be one of `exploring`, `proposed`, `approved`, `archived` or `current`, read from the Hub's database row and never from the file, and SHALL change only along exploring to proposed, proposed to approved, proposed to exploring, approved to exploring, approved to archived, exploring to archived and proposed to archived; any other move, a move to the phase the document already holds, and any move to or from `current` SHALL be refused naming why.

WhyThe status line in the file is a copy for its reader. Reading phase from a row the Hub alone writes is what makes approval impossible to forge by editing the file.

Behaviour

Phase transitions

  1. A new document starts in exploring; a capability document starts in current and never moves.
  2. exploring to proposed: operator only, exploration closed, no blocking finding.
  3. proposed to approved: operator only, no blocking finding (stale imports and delivery excepted).
  4. proposed to exploring and approved to exploring: the operator reopens; exploration must be closed again.
  5. approved to archived: operator only; for a change-spec, every task approved or rejected and a merge or a stated no_capability_change.
  6. exploring or proposed to archived: operator only, and only while the document has no requirements and no tasks.
  7. archived: no transition out.

Design

D1 Phase and rigor live on the database row; the file carries a rendered copy. Editing the file can never approve, gate or ungate a document.

D2 Operator-only acts (approve, archive, rigor, proposal decisions, closing exploration) are refused in the service function that performs them, not only at the route, and the agent surface has no field that expresses them.

D3 Completeness is computed by the Hub inside the transition and reported whole; a save with findings is still written, because a document under discussion is incomplete by definition.

D4 Identity is key-correlated and Hub-minted, kept in an `aw_identity` block the Hub overwrites on every write, with a persisted high-water mark so identifiers are never reused.

D5 At contract or gate rigor a submission becomes per-unit proposals compared by key and guarded by the content digest; decisions use a conditional update so exactly one wins.

D6 A capability is written only through a merge, and a fold is a merge the Hub drafts and then archives the change; archiving an approved change requires its tasks decided and a fold or a stated reason. Deletion is for documents that never shipped and that no merge cites.

D7 Reads are bounded by the Hub, and an oversized read goes to a file in the run's workspace.
