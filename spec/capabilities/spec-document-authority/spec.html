Summary

Where a specification document lives, who may write it, how its requirements are identified, what phase and rigor it is in, and who may move it. The document is a file in the project; an agent submits a structured payload and the Hub renders it, so the format is a schema rather than a request, and phase and rigor are held by the Hub and changed only by the operator. Approving a document creates the tasks it declares and reports what it did; a finished change reaches the capability corpus through an audited merge (a fold), and a document can be archived, retired or, if it never shipped, deleted.

Problem

A specification is only authoritative if nobody can forge its approval, quietly rewrite what was approved, or change the record of current behaviour without saying where the change came from. An agent can write any file in its checkout, so every one of those guarantees has to be held by the Hub, not asked of the author.

Scope

In scope

  • The document file, its structured payload, requirement identity, digests and divergence
  • The attributed event history of every change, and the `spec_updated` broadcast
  • Phase and its transitions, completeness checks, approval and archive authority
  • Rigor and the proposals a contract or gate document records instead of applying edits
  • Naming, renaming, reading (by operator and agent, bounded) and rendering a document
  • Declared tasks: title, files and the submission's advisory warnings about them
  • Creating and refreshing declared tasks at approval, and the approval report
  • Writing capability documents through merges and folds; retiring a capability; deleting a document

Non-goals

  • Discovery, `spec/index.json` and the corpus map (spec-corpus-map); adopting untracked files (spec-document-adoption)
  • Which documents an agent may start (agent-document-creation)
  • What a turn with a document open is told and which tools it gets (spec-chat-session)
  • A document's flow delivery (agent-flows) and cross-document task imports and dependency edges (task-dependencies)
  • Task status transitions once a task exists (task-lifecycle-governance)
  • Evidence, footprints, coverage and drift (requirement-traceability); roadmaps and their slices (spec-roadmaps)

Requirements

FR-40# Requirement 1 of 72

SHALL Specification documents SHALL be files beneath the project's working directory, read from disk on every request, with every path resolved through the project workspace, which refuses absolute paths, traversal segments, control characters and symlink escapes.

WhyThe file is the document. The Hub keeps no second copy of its content and has no route by which a client pushes one, so there is nothing to reconcile.

FR-41# Requirement 2 of 72

SHALL An agent SHALL author a specification only by submitting a structured payload; the Hub SHALL validate it, render the document's markup itself and write it, and SHALL refuse an invalid payload with an error naming the offending field, writing nothing.

WhyA schema the agent cannot violate replaces a markup contract it is merely asked to honour.

FR-42# Requirement 3 of 72

SHALL Every payload SHALL declare `schema_version`; a missing or unsupported version SHALL be refused by name, and a field a part does not define SHALL be kept unchanged across a read and a rewrite of the document.

WhyA rewrite must lose nothing the current schema cannot name. Keeping a field is not reading it: see the unknown_field finding.

FR-121# Requirement 4 of 72

SHALL A payload in the shape `read_spec_document` returns SHALL be accepted as a submission without loss: criteria nested under a requirement join the top-level list under that requirement's key, the read-only `identifier`, `state` and `anchor` fields are dropped and a retired requirement is left out; a requirement from a truncated read, or a nested criterion that conflicts with a top-level one, SHALL be refused.

WhyAgents edit by reading a document and resubmitting it. Resubmitted as read, the nested criteria were kept as unknown fields and the top-level list was empty, so every criterion was deleted (F452).

FR-43# Requirement 5 of 72

MUST The Hub SHALL assign each requirement its identifier (`FR-<n>`), correlated across submissions by the requirement's key; an identifier SHALL stay with its key whatever the text, position or schema version, SHALL never be reused after its requirement is removed, and an identifier or identity block supplied in a submission SHALL be ignored.

WhyTasks and evidence point at identifiers. Correlating by position would renumber every requirement after an insertion and silently re-target what points at them.

FR-48# Requirement 6 of 72

SHALL The Hub SHALL store a content digest for every document it writes and a digest for each requirement's text, and SHALL never resolve a file that differs from its recorded digest on its own initiative: a write submitted over a diverged file replaces it and reports the divergence in its answer.

WhyChoosing between a hand edit and the recorded version is the operator's decision; the Hub surfaces both digests and waits.

FR-96# Requirement 7 of 72

SHALL Serving a document whose file no longer matches the digest the Hub recorded SHALL mark it as diverged, with both digests, in the operator's document read, in the document listing and in an agent's read, without refusing the read.

WhyThe phase is read from a row, but the content is served from the file; unmarked, approval would attach to a path rather than to the bytes anyone reads. Editing on the way to a new revision is legitimate, so the read is marked, not refused.

FR-47# Requirement 8 of 72

SHALL The Hub SHALL append an event for every change to a document's content, phase, path, rendering or merge record, naming the actor, its origin (operator control, agent submission, or Hub lifecycle) and the run where there is one; events SHALL never be edited or deleted.

WhyThe history is the record of who did what to a document; an editable record is not one.

FR-80# Requirement 9 of 72

SHALL Every content write SHALL record its actor as established from the caller's credential (the operator for a project credential, the agent and its run for a run credential) and never from anything in the request body.

WhyAn operator's write must be distinguishable from an agent's, so a corpus written by hand is never mistaken for one an agent authored, and an actor a caller can state is an actor a caller can invent.

FR-44# Requirement 10 of 72

SHALL A document's phase SHALL be one of `exploring`, `proposed`, `approved`, `archived` or `current`, read from the Hub's database row and never from the file, and SHALL change only along exploring to proposed, proposed to approved, proposed to exploring, approved to exploring, approved to archived, exploring to archived and proposed to archived; any other move, a move to the phase the document already holds, and any move to or from `current` SHALL be refused naming why.

WhyThe status line in the file is a copy for its reader. Reading phase from a row the Hub alone writes is what makes approval impossible to forge by editing the file.

FR-45# Requirement 11 of 72

SHALL Approving and archiving a document SHALL be refused to any actor but the operator, enforced in the Hub's phase-transition function itself so every caller of it is bound.

WhyA rule checked at one surface survives only until a second caller is added.

FR-122# Requirement 12 of 72

SHALL Proposing, approving, reopening and archiving a document, and declaring its exploration closed, SHALL be reachable only through operator routes; an agent's tool surface SHALL offer no field or route that expresses any of them, and closing exploration SHALL also be refused to a non-operator actor in the service.

WhyWhether an exploration is complete and whether a document is agreed are the operator's judgements. An agent writes and reads; it cannot move its own document forward.

FR-123# Requirement 13 of 72

SHALL Proposing a document SHALL answer with a `proposed` field that is true only when the document moved to `proposed`; a document that cannot move yet SHALL be answered with `proposed: false` and every blocker, not with an error status.

Why"Not yet" is a normal answer, and a 200 alone cannot say whether the document moved (F528).

FR-46# Requirement 14 of 72

MUST The Hub SHALL evaluate a document's completeness itself whenever it is moved to `proposed` or `approved`, inside the phase-transition function, and SHALL refuse the move listing every blocker in one answer (for `proposed`, including that exploration has not been closed); a move the phase map or the actor forbids SHALL be refused as such, not reported as incompleteness.

WhyA check the author reports passing is not a check. A proposed document can still be edited, and a document can be adopted at proposed, so having been proposed does not prove completeness at approval.

FR-124# Requirement 15 of 72

SHALL The blocking findings SHALL be: no requirements (for a roadmap, no slices); a requirement no acceptance criterion names; a requirement neither a declared task nor a board task serves; a task naming no requirement; empty non-goals; an unresolved open question; a clarification marker; a `depends_on` naming no task; a dependency cycle; an import from a document that is not approved; an unknown field; a slice whose `done` states no outcome; a slice document whose roadmap is not approved or lacks the slice; and, on a change document, an absent or incomplete delivery. A capability document SHALL NOT be asked for tasks or non-goals.

WhyOne list, so an author learns everything a proposal would refuse in one answer. A capability states shipped behaviour; the changes folded into it carry the tasks and non-goals (F534).

FR-125# Requirement 16 of 72

SHALL At approval the Hub SHALL NOT block on an import naming a document that is no longer approved, nor on an absent or incomplete delivery: the import is kept and reported, and the delivery is treated as no flow.

WhyA document proposed in good faith should not be held hostage by another document being reopened, nor by a delivery answer that only decides whether a flow is started.

FR-126# Requirement 17 of 72

SHALL A field that a requirement, acceptance criterion, task or slice does not define SHALL be reported as a blocking `unknown_field` finding naming the field and saying nothing reads it, and, for acceptance criteria nested under a requirement, saying where they belong.

WhyThe payload keeps unknown fields so a rewrite loses nothing, so a field written in the wrong place is stored and never read; an agent nested its criteria and was then told there were none (F502).

FR-127# Requirement 18 of 72

SHALL The answer to a submission SHALL carry its `blocking` findings and `ready_to_propose`, false while any blocking finding remains; a submission with findings is still written.

WhyA document under discussion is incomplete by definition, so findings do not refuse a save; but a saved document is not a ready one, and an agent read a bare success as "ready" (F502).

FR-128# Requirement 19 of 72

SHALL A content submission to an `approved` document SHALL be refused, from any actor, until the operator reopens the document.

WhySilently rewriting what the operator approved would make the approval meaningless; reopening is the recorded way to change it.

FR-73# Requirement 20 of 72

SHALL Archiving SHALL change only the document's phase, keeping its requirements, digests, events and tasks, and an `archived` document SHALL have no further phase transition.

WhyArchived is history: it records what shipped, and nothing should be able to walk it back.

FR-97# Requirement 21 of 72

SHALL A document in `exploring` or `proposed` SHALL be archivable by the operator only while it has no requirements and no tasks; otherwise the move SHALL be refused naming that work exists.

WhyA document created by mistake must be retirable through the phase machine, but archiving must never become a way to bury work that exists.

FR-113# Requirement 22 of 72

MUST Archiving an approved change-spec MUST be refused, naming what is missing, unless every task the document declared is approved or rejected and either a merge names the change or the operator states `no_capability_change` with a non-empty reason.

WhyArchived has no way back; a shipped change whose requirements live in no capability must not quietly become history.

FR-94# Requirement 23 of 72

SHALL The Hub SHALL refuse to rename a document that has ever been approved, recorded durably at first approval and never cleared by archiving or reopening.

WhyOnce other documents and tasks refer to a document by path, the path is a promise; archive-then-rename must not break it.

FR-74# Requirement 24 of 72

SHALL A document created with kind `capability` SHALL start in `current` and SHALL refuse every phase transition; its content SHALL be written only by the operator; and every document's `kind` SHALL be fixed at creation, so a submission whose `kind` differs SHALL be refused without reclassifying the document.

WhyA capability describes current shipped behaviour; it has no exploration to close and nothing to propose.

FR-61# Requirement 25 of 72

SHALL A document created without a path SHALL be given a minted `spec/changes/<colour>-<mythic animal>/spec.html` path, drawn at random and derived from nothing about the document, free of every recorded document and existing file, found by a bounded search, and returned to the caller.

WhyA document explores because its subject is not yet known; a name that is obviously arbitrary cannot be misread as a considered one.

FR-62# Requirement 26 of 72

SHALL The Hub SHALL rename a document from a prose subject, deriving `spec/changes/<slug>/spec.html` and never accepting a path, moving the file, updating the recorded path and every undelivered queue entry naming it, recording a `renamed` event with both paths, and leaving its identity, identifiers, content, digests, past events and the index file unchanged; it SHALL refuse, before moving anything, a document ever approved, a subject that yields no slug, and a path another document or file occupies.

WhyDeriving the slug makes a traversal, a hidden segment or another filename unexpressible rather than merely rejected; the index is the operator's file, and an entry naming the moved document reports as missing.

FR-57# Requirement 27 of 72

SHALL A rename SHALL make the subject the document's title.

WhyA document is renamed because its subject became clear; keeping the placeholder title would contradict its own path on every list.

FR-104# Requirement 28 of 72

SHALL An agent's read of a document SHALL fit a fixed bound the Hub enforces (25,000 characters): requirements are returned in identifier order up to the bound, with the identifiers (or keys) and sections left out named and how to read them; the first requested item is always returned, cut if alone too large; an agent MAY read named requirements only, which omits the preamble, or an outline without rationale or criteria; an unknown identifier is reported, not failed.

WhyA result larger than the harness accepts is no answer at all, and the agent falls back to a paraphrase, which the read exists to prevent.

FR-105# Requirement 29 of 72

SHALL A read that does not fit the bound SHALL be written into the reading run's own workspace under `.agentweave/reads/`, as text a file tool shows line by line, and answered with that file's path and the requirements it holds; where the run has no workspace or the place is not a plain directory, the bounded read SHALL be returned instead, and the file SHALL be kept out of every commit.

WhyA shell tool spills above 30,000 characters, so a run without MCP needs the read as a file it can page through.

FR-106# Requirement 30 of 72

SHALL An agent's read SHALL accept a document's `spdoc-` id wherever it accepts a path, resolving it only within the agent's own project, where an id from another project reads exactly like an unknown one.

WhyTasks carry the document's id, so that is what an agent holding a task reaches for.

FR-63# Requirement 31 of 72

SHALL The rendered document SHALL list acceptance criteria grouped in requirement order, keeping the submitted order within a requirement, and SHALL state "None outstanding" when there are no open questions.

WhyA reader scans the criteria table by requirement; and an absent section cannot be told apart from questions asked and resolved.

FR-76# Requirement 32 of 72

MUST The rendered document SHALL take its neutral colours from the same inherited CSS custom properties (`--bg`, `--fg`, `--muted`, `--border`, `--surface`, `--surface-2`) the embedding shell sets for the Hub's theme, keeping its own semantic hues, with no external resource reference.

WhyA second, independently named set of neutrals is one the shell's theming cannot reach; the document must still open correctly on its own.

FR-77# Requirement 33 of 72

SHALL A rendered requirement SHALL show its modal obligation with a treatment distinct for each obligation and consistent across requirements sharing it.

WhyA reader should tell requirement strength by scanning, not by reading every word.

FR-64# Requirement 34 of 72

SHALL A document SHALL have a rigor of `sketch`, `contract` or `gate`, held by the Hub on the document's row, `sketch` by default and shown in the rendered document; each change SHALL be written against the document's current content digest and recorded append-only with the previous and new level, actor, reason and digest.

WhyRigor is not phase: phase asks whether the operator agreed to the document, rigor what happens to work that does not satisfy it. `gate` refuses approval of work while a requirement it serves is unverified.

FR-65# Requirement 35 of 72

SHALL Only the operator SHALL change a document's rigor, enforced in the rigor function itself; an agent SHALL have no argument, tool or route that expresses a promotion or demotion, and the operator's demotion SHALL be recorded and attributed.

WhyA gate the blocked party can lower is not a gate; an operator who needs past one has a visible, recorded way through.

FR-66# Requirement 36 of 72

SHALL Promotion to `contract` or `gate` SHALL be refused while the document has unresolved identifiers, duplicate references or content that does not parse; demotion SHALL NOT be so conditioned and SHALL change enforcement only, keeping links, revisions, evidence and reviews.

WhyRigor is a claim about enforceability; a demotion that destroyed the record would launder unverified work.

FR-102# Requirement 37 of 72

SHALL The document's view SHALL list every rigor change (levels, actor, reason, time, newest first), and the app SHALL ask for a reason whenever it changes rigor and SHALL NOT submit a demotion without one.

WhyA demotion is legitimate because it is recorded; a record nobody can read, or whose reason is always blank, is the unrecorded override in another form.

FR-67# Requirement 38 of 72

SHALL At `contract` or `gate` rigor a submission SHALL NOT change the live document; the Hub SHALL diff it against the stored payload and record one pending proposal per added, modified or removed requirement (matched by key) and one for all non-requirement content, recording nothing for an unchanged submission; at `sketch` a submission applies immediately.

WhyWhether an edit applies or waits is the document's property, never the caller's choice; one tool and one payload shape serve both.

FR-68# Requirement 39 of 72

SHALL Pending proposals SHALL be shown on the document's own view, one row per requirement key plus one for the summary and other non-requirement content, and deciding one SHALL NOT change the status of any other.

WhyA pending change must be discoverable without leaving the document, yet kept apart from the live text so a proposed statement is not mistaken for the document's own.

FR-69# Requirement 40 of 72

SHALL An accepted proposal's record SHALL name its proposer (actor and run) and the operator who accepted it in separate fields.

WhyThe content event names who wrote the file; the proposal row keeps who proposed it, so neither identity is lost.

FR-70# Requirement 41 of 72

SHALL Accepting, rejecting or withdrawing a proposal SHALL be refused to any actor but the operator, enforced in the functions that perform them.

WhyThe same construction as approval: an agent cannot accept its own edit into a gated document.

FR-71# Requirement 42 of 72

SHALL Accepting a proposal SHALL be refused, and the proposal marked `stale`, when the document's content digest has changed since the proposal was recorded, or since the digest the operator supplies.

WhyA proposal must never be applied to content it was not compared against.

FR-103# Requirement 43 of 72

SHALL A repeated identical submission from the same proposer SHALL record no new proposal and name the pending one; a proposer's newest submission SHALL supersede its own earlier pending proposal for any unit it now changes differently or leaves as stored; the operator MAY withdraw a pending proposal without judging it; a proposal SHALL leave `pending` at most once, and each decision SHALL be broadcast to the project's open views.

WhyRetries and revisions are ordinary; without this the list fills with twins only a false rejection could clear. Different proposers' proposals are alternatives and never supersede each other.

FR-53# Requirement 44 of 72

SHALL Every change to a document's content, phase, path, placement, proposals or the index SHALL be broadcast to the project's subscribers as `spec_updated`.

WhyAn open view should reflect the change without a manual refresh.

FR-58# Requirement 45 of 72

SHALL A declared task MAY state its `title`, used when the task is created; otherwise the title SHALL be derived from the description's first sentence, kept to 80 characters and ending on a whole word, with a description already that short used unchanged.

WhyA board shows names, and a whole sentence of intent is not one.

FR-59# Requirement 46 of 72

SHALL A task created or refreshed from a declared entry SHALL carry, as text lines, the acceptance criteria of exactly the requirements the entry names (by name, resolved or not), grouped in the document's requirement order and keeping each criterion's handle; a criterion with no given, when or then SHALL be left out, and criteria that cannot be read SHALL never stop a task being created.

WhyThe turn composed for a task shows its criteria to the builder and the reviewer as the standard; a task without them is judged against the code instead.

FR-107# Requirement 47 of 72

MAY A declared task MAY carry a `files` list of repo-relative paths it expects to edit, where a directory covers every path beneath it; an empty or absolute path SHALL be refused naming the task.

WhyThe planner's way to say which tasks touch the same file.

FR-108# Requirement 48 of 72

MUST When two local tasks of one document declare overlapping `files` and neither reaches the other through `depends_on`, the submission's answer MUST carry an advisory `unordered_file_overlap` warning naming both keys and a shared path, without affecting `ready_to_propose`.

WhyParallel tasks editing one file conflicted at merge (F509).

FR-110# Requirement 49 of 72

MUST When a document declares two or more local tasks and at least one declares no `files`, the submission's answer MUST carry one advisory `undeclared_task_files` warning naming each such task, without affecting `ready_to_propose`.

WhyA planner that declares no files defeats the overlap warning silently (F509).

FR-129# Requirement 50 of 72

SHOULD When a local task's `files` are all tests and another local task depends on it, the submission's answer SHOULD carry an advisory `test_only_task` warning saying to put the failing test and its fix in one task.

WhyA test written red for a later task to turn green fails the project's checks at approval and lands only by override (F512).

FR-79# Requirement 51 of 72

SHALL The operator SHALL be able to write the content of any document other than a capability directly, under the same payload contract, validation and refusals as an agent's submission, with a `contract` or `gate` document recording pending proposals instead.

WhyThe operator's own authority over a document needs a route, not an in-process call.

FR-114# Requirement 52 of 72

MUST A capability document's content MUST change only through a merge: a direct content write to one SHALL be refused naming the merge route, and a merge naming no change SHALL be accepted as an operator edit and recorded as one.

WhyEvery capability change then has a history entry saying which change it came from, or that it was an edit.

FR-75# Requirement 53 of 72

SHALL The operator SHALL be able to merge a payload into a capability document naming the approved or archived change documents it absorbs, recording one merge row per change and a `merged` event, and the merge SHALL be retrievable from either document; a target that is not a capability, or a source that is not approved or archived, SHALL be refused before anything is written.

WhyA merge cites finished work, attributed to the operator.

FR-111# Requirement 54 of 72

MUST For an approved change and a named capability, the Hub MUST return a fold draft without writing: the capability's payload with each change requirement appended under `<change-slug>-<key>` (at most 64 characters), its criteria re-pointed at the new key, and every draft key the capability already holds reported as a collision rather than overwritten.

WhyThe operator reviews drafted text instead of writing it; most close-outs copy the change's statements unchanged.

FR-117# Requirement 55 of 72

MUST The fold draft MUST list the capability's current requirements (key and statement) and criteria (key, requirement and then), in the capability's order.

WhyThe operator retires by key, so the keys must be in front of them.

FR-112# Requirement 56 of 72

MUST The operator MUST be able to fold an approved change into a capability in one request, choosing which requirements to fold (default all), each optionally with an edited key, statement or modal or replacing a named capability requirement; the fold writes through the merge and, unless told not to, archives the change in the same transaction; it MUST be refused before anything is written when the change is not approved, a task of it is open, a named requirement is not the change's, a key collides without naming what it replaces, a replaced key is not the capability's, or the target is not a capability.

WhyOne audited operator action replaces per-change reconcile scripts; a change touching two capabilities is folded twice, archiving on the second.

FR-116# Requirement 57 of 72

MUST A fold MUST accept capability requirement keys and criterion keys to retire, writing the capability without them in the same merge (a retired requirement takes its criteria), and MUST be refused before writing when a key is not the capability's or a requirement is both replaced and retired.

WhyA change that narrows or supersedes behaviour must say so in the corpus through the same audited step.

FR-115# Requirement 58 of 72

MUST The read of an approved or archived change MUST carry `fold_state` (`tasks_open`, `ready`, or `folded` with the capabilities it went into), and the app's phase bar MUST show a ready change as shipped but not yet in a capability, offering Fold into capability (capability picker, the draft's requirements with editable key and statement, archive on by default, confirm) and Archive without a capability change, which asks for a reason.

WhyThe close-out is something the operator does where they already look.

FR-118# Requirement 59 of 72

MUST The fold dialog MUST offer the capability's requirements and criteria for retirement, filterable by text, and send the chosen keys with the fold.

WhyRetiring stays something the operator does in the app.

FR-119# Requirement 60 of 72

MUST The operator MUST be able to delete a document that is not a capability, not archived and named by no merge, removing its file, its index entry, its requirements with their revisions, evidence and links, its events, its tasks as a task delete does, and its stopped flows; it MUST be refused, writing nothing, for a capability, an archived or folded document, a document with a run active on one of its tasks, or one whose flow is still running.

WhyDrive residue and abandoned drafts otherwise stay on the board and in coverage forever; what shipped, or what the corpus cites, is kept.

FR-120# Requirement 61 of 72

MUST The app MUST offer Delete for a deletable document on its phase bar, behind a confirm naming what goes with it, and show the Hub's refusal when there is one.

WhyCleanup should never need a database script.

FR-133# Requirement 62 of 72

MUST The operator MUST be able to move a capability from current to archived, stating a reason and optionally naming the current capability that absorbed it; the move MUST be refused without a reason, when the named capability is not another current capability, when the actor is not the operator, and while a task that is neither approved nor rejected links one of its active requirements.

WhyA corpus that cannot lose a capability keeps describing features that do not exist. Open work against it must be decided first, or that work would serve requirements nobody holds any more.

FR-134# Requirement 63 of 72

MUST Every requirement of an archived capability MUST be retired when it is archived and MUST stay retired whenever the document is indexed again.

WhyCoverage, linking and evidence all read the requirement index; a retired capability's requirements must leave all three at once, and a reindex from the unchanged file must not bring them back.

FR-135# Requirement 64 of 72

MUST A merge or fold into an archived capability MUST be refused, writing nothing.

WhyThe file is the record of what the capability said when it was retired.

FR-136# Requirement 65 of 72

MUST Retiring MUST record a phase event carrying the reason and the absorbing capability, re-render the file with its archived status, and keep the document in spec/index.json with status archived.

WhyWhy a capability left, and where its behaviour went, must be readable later from the document's own history.

FR-137# Requirement 66 of 72

MUST A current capability's view in the app MUST offer Retire, asking for the reason and optionally the absorbing capability, and MUST show the Hub's refusal when one is returned.

WhyRetiring is an operator decision made where the operator reads the capability.

FR-138# Requirement 67 of 72

SHALL Approving a document SHALL create the tasks it declares that do not exist yet, each unassigned, in the entry status and linked to the requirements it names, idempotently per document and declared key; a declared task naming a requirement the document does not resolve SHALL still be created with the reference kept as unresolved, and a document declaring no tasks SHALL create none without error.

WhyA document that declares its own decomposition and produces nothing leaves the operator to re-describe it by hand. Who performs the work is not the document's decision.

FR-139# Requirement 68 of 72

MUST Every approval of a document MUST record with it a report of what approval did: tasks created; declared tasks not created because existing work already serves their requirements; dependencies not honoured and why; whether creating the board failed and why (distinct from declaring no tasks); and whether a flow was created, naming it, or why not (for a non-change document, that only a change document declares how it is built). The report MUST be returned and shown on the document, the last one recorded winning; where no flow was created it MUST point to the document's single start-flow control while no unarchived flow declares the document. A board failure MUST NOT undo the approval and MUST leave none of the tasks it created.

WhyWhat approval did to the board must be visible where the operator decided it, and a half-built board the report does not list is worse than none.

FR-140# Requirement 69 of 72

MUST When a document is approved again, a task it created earlier that is still declared under the same key and is neither `approved` nor `rejected` MUST take the entry's current title, description and acceptance criteria and link to exactly the requirements of this document the entry names; its status, assignee, priority and links to other documents' requirements MUST NOT change. An `approved` or `rejected` task MUST NOT be changed by a later approval.

WhyA revision that changes what is asked must reach the open work, or coverage shows a new requirement unserved while a task claims a retired one; progress and assignment stay the board's, and a closed task records what was asked when it was decided.

FR-141# Requirement 70 of 72

MUST The approval report MUST list each refreshed task with the fields that changed and the requirement links added and removed, each closed task that links a requirement this document has retired, and each task this document created whose key it no longer declares; none of the latter two MUST be changed.

WhyAnything a re-approval changed or chose not to change must be visible where the operator decided it.

FR-142# Requirement 71 of 72

MUST The app's approval report MUST show refreshed tasks, closed tasks linking a retired requirement, and tasks no longer declared.

WhyThe report is where the operator sees what approval did to the board.

FR-143# Requirement 72 of 72

SHALL The agent API SHALL let an agent read a specification document of its project, by path or by document id, in every phase, as structured content rather than rendered markup, and the operation SHALL be in the surface the agent is told it has.

WhyAn agent's isolated checkout predates the document, so without this it implements from someone else's paraphrase.

Behaviour

Phase transitions

  1. A new document starts in exploring; a capability document starts in current and never moves.
  2. exploring to proposed: operator only, exploration closed, no blocking finding.
  3. proposed to approved: operator only, no blocking finding (stale imports and delivery excepted).
  4. proposed to exploring and approved to exploring: the operator reopens; exploration must be closed again.
  5. approved to archived: operator only; for a change-spec, every task approved or rejected and a merge or a stated no_capability_change.
  6. exploring or proposed to archived: operator only, and only while the document has no requirements and no tasks.
  7. archived: no transition out.

Design

D1 Phase and rigor live on the database row; the file carries a rendered copy. Editing the file can never approve, gate or ungate a document.

D2 Operator-only acts (approve, archive, rigor, proposal decisions, closing exploration) are refused in the service function that performs them, not only at the route, and the agent surface has no field that expresses them.

D3 Completeness is computed by the Hub inside the transition and reported whole; a save with findings is still written, because a document under discussion is incomplete by definition.

D4 Identity is key-correlated and Hub-minted, kept in an `aw_identity` block the Hub overwrites on every write, with a persisted high-water mark so identifiers are never reused.

D5 At contract or gate rigor a submission becomes per-unit proposals compared by key and guarded by the content digest; decisions use a conditional update so exactly one wins.

D6 A capability is written only through a merge, and a fold is a merge the Hub drafts and then archives the change; archiving an approved change requires its tasks decided and a fold or a stated reason. Deletion is for documents that never shipped and that no merge cites.

D7 Reads are bounded by the Hub, and an oversized read goes to a file in the run's workspace.
