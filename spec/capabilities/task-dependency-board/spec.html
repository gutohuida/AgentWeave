Task dependency board

capabilitycurrentsketch

10 MUST·10 requirements·25 criteria

Summary

A second view of the Tasks area that lays one document's tasks out by dependency depth, draws the edges between them, shows each card's status and why it cannot start, collapses finished layers, and names prerequisites that live in other documents.

Problem

The status board shows what is in flight but not what can start or what is waiting on what, so a stalled decomposition looks the same whether it waits on work, on review or on a rejection.

Scope

In scope

  • the Dependencies view, its document picker and layout
  • the board read routes

Non-goals

  • declaring or removing dependencies (task-dependencies)
  • the status board itself

Requirements

FR-1# Requirement 1 of 10

SHALL The dependency board SHALL place each task in a layer by its longest-path depth among this board's tasks — tasks with no on-board prerequisite at the top, each task below the deepest of its prerequisites — and SHALL draw a directed line for every edge whose two ends are on screen.

WhyDepth goes on the vertical axis because chain length is unbounded while parallel width is bounded by the agent budget, and vertical scrolling is cheap. Longest path keeps a task with several prerequisites below all of them; the structure is a DAG, not a tree.

FR-2# Requirement 2 of 10

SHALL Each task on the dependency board SHALL show its own status on its card.

WhyOn the status board a column states status; here position states depth, so status must live on the card or be lost.

FR-3# Requirement 3 of 10

SHALL The dependency board SHALL offer no control that creates, removes or alters a dependency, and SHALL state, above the board, where the selected board's dependencies are changed.

WhyThe board is a read of the graph; a drag-to-link affordance would let structure change without the document or an explicit operator declaration recording it. Saying where structure comes from spares the operator discovering the absence by trying.

FR-4# Requirement 4 of 10

SHALL Where a task cannot start, the board SHALL distinguish waiting on prerequisites still being worked (`gated`), waiting on prerequisites that are done but not yet approved (`waiting_on_review`: every unmet prerequisite is completed or under_review), and waiting on a rejected prerequisite (`gated_on_rejected`), both on the card and as a per-layer summary; a started task whose prerequisite left approved SHALL carry its own mark instead.

WhyAll three look like a card that will not start but have different remedies: wait, get it reviewed, or decide about abandoned work. Because a dependency is met only at approval, an unattended review backlog stalls every layer below it, and must not read as the dependency feature being broken.

FR-5# Requirement 5 of 10

SHALL The dependency board SHALL show one board at a time, chosen from a picker listing every document that has tasks plus a standing "No document" board for tasks that belong to none, each entry showing outstanding (not approved or rejected) over total tasks; the picker SHALL default to the first document board.

WhyA project's finished work grows without bound; one document's decomposition does not. Hand-made tasks must be reachable on a board of their own. Showing what remains on the picker makes choosing a board and seeing its progress one act.

FR-6# Requirement 6 of 10

SHALL A layer whose tasks are all approved or rejected SHALL be collapsed by default to a toggle naming how many tasks are done and how many links it hides, which the operator can expand; edges with one end in a collapsed layer SHALL be drawn as stubs to that toggle.

WhyCollapsing keeps a finished document's board short while the graph's shape stays legible; hiding the tasks or their edges entirely would leave the remaining graph looking rootless.

FR-7# Requirement 7 of 10

SHALL A prerequisite that belongs to another board SHALL be shown as a distinct reference naming its title and owning document, not laid out as a task of this board, and selecting it SHALL switch to that document's board.

WhyThe foreign task is not this document's work to act on, but hiding it would make a gated task look gated by nothing. Naming and linking the owning document makes the blocker reachable.

FR-8# Requirement 8 of 10

SHALL The Tasks area SHALL keep the status board as its default view and offer the dependency board as a second view the operator switches to.

WhyA status board answers what is in flight; a dependency board answers what can start. Neither contains the other.

FR-9# Requirement 9 of 10

SHALL Hovering a connected task SHALL highlight its full upstream and downstream lineage and dim unrelated work, and a card SHALL hint at the trace only when it has at least one edge.

WhyIn a dense graph the eye cannot follow lines; tracing a chain on hover answers what a task waits on and what waits on it without opening anything.

FR-10# Requirement 10 of 10

SHALL `GET /tasks/board` SHALL return one board's tasks (with dependency fields) and the edges from them in one call, and `GET /tasks/boards` SHALL return each board's document id, title, total and outstanding count.

WhyThe layout needs the edge set once, not reconstructed per card, and must not issue a query per card.

Design

D1 Depth downward. Longest-path depth over on-board edges only; off-board prerequisites add no depth. A cycle (which the writer refuses) yields depth 0 rather than hanging. D2 One board per document, plus a standing no-document board. Outstanding counts exclude approved and rejected. D3 Stall states derive from the read model's dependency_state and each prerequisite's status; no second fetch. D4 Read-only. No authoring affordance on the board; structure is changed in the document or through the operator's dependency routes.
