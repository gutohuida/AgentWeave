Loop firing accountability

capabilitycurrentsketch

11 MUST·11 requirements·30 criteria

Summary

A job's firing records say truthfully what each firing did: a firing whose agent cannot start is recorded as failed at once, a stranded record never makes a loop read as firing, a refused firing leaves no conversation behind, and the firing count counts firings rather than agents started.

Problem

An unattended loop is read through its firing records and counts. Records left in progress, empty conversations from refused firings, and counts that mix firings with dispatches make a stalled loop look busy and a busy one look broken.

Scope

In scope

  • The status of a firing's run record from queueing to the end of its run
  • Reconciling stranded firing records
  • Which firings create conversations
  • The job's firing count

Non-goals

  • What a firing decides to do (agent-loops)

Requirements

FR-1# Requirement 1 of 11

SHALL When a firing has queued its input and the Hub then learns that the agent's turn cannot begin (for example, no runner is bound), the Hub SHALL record that firing as `failed` at once, carrying the reason the inbound queue gives for that agent, and SHALL NOT leave it `in_progress`.

WhyThe reason exists at the moment the turn is refused; a firing left in progress made a loop read as firing continuously with nothing behind it.

FR-2# Requirement 2 of 11

SHALL The Hub SHALL NOT report a loop as firing on account of a firing record that has no running run behind it, and SHALL mark every such record `failed` when the Hub next starts, leaving alone a record whose run is still running or whose input waits on a provider-allowance refusal.

WhyOnce a refused turn is recorded at the moment it happens, a record can only be stranded by the Hub process dying, and a start always follows that. The loop's active-firing flag is derived from live runs, so it is honest without waiting for the start (operator decision, 2026-08-21).

FR-3# Requirement 3 of 11

SHALL A firing that is refused or skipped before it queues any input (busy guard, stall, stop condition, every task already in flight, held-queue coalescing) SHALL NOT create a conversation; a firing that proceeds SHALL create, or resume, a conversation named after its job.

WhyA conversation implies work happened; refused firings on a short schedule otherwise fill the list with empty threads indistinguishable by name from real ones.

FR-4# Requirement 4 of 11

SHALL A job's firing count SHALL rise by exactly one for each firing that queues input for at least one agent, however many agents it starts, and SHALL NOT rise for a firing that queues nothing; each agent a firing starts SHALL have its own run record and conversation, and the job's card SHALL label the count as fired rather than as runs.

WhyThe count rises where input is queued, so a firing whose turn then fails to begin still counts once and its record says why. A run in the app means one agent's attempt at a turn.

FR-5# Requirement 5 of 11

SHALL Each firing record SHALL name the conversation the firing used, so its output, questions and bound task are reachable from the record.

WhyTimestamps are not a correlation.

FR-6# Requirement 6 of 11

MUST A firing refused by the busy guard, or one whose every candidate is already in flight, SHALL create no run record; a stalled firing SHALL create one record, and further firings with the same reason SHALL increment that record's tick count; a firing skipped because the loop stopped SHALL create a record.

WhyA loop ticking without working must not bury the firings that did work within the bounded run history.

FR-7# Requirement 7 of 11

SHALL Every reason stored on a run record SHALL fit the 500 characters the history returns; a reason that names a remedy SHALL be shortened in what it quotes rather than in the remedy, and a continuing stall SHALL be recognised by its shortened reason.

WhyOne over-long row would otherwise make the whole history fail to load for as long as it is among the rows returned.

FR-8# Requirement 8 of 11

SHALL A plain job's firing SHALL be recorded as skipped and queue nothing when its agent is held by a provider usage-allowance refusal and an earlier firing of the same job still has input queued for that agent; consecutive such firings SHALL count into one record.

WhyA job's message is a standing instruction: one queued copy delivers it at the reset, and more would deliver it once per tick. The first firing during a hold still queues, so the instruction is never lost.

FR-9# Requirement 9 of 11

SHALL A manual firing of a job SHALL answer from a run record only if that firing wrote it or counted into it (409 for skipped, 500 for failed, with the record's reason); otherwise, for a loop, it SHALL answer a busy-guard refusal with 409 naming the busy reason and the first condition that held (empty queue, documentless loop, no other agent free), an all-in-flight queue with 409 (naming any held agent and when its hold ends), and anything else as 500 `Failed to fire job`.

WhyThe busy guard records nothing, so the newest record is usually an earlier firing's and is not this press's answer. A record's requester is changed only on a record the press wrote.

FR-10# Requirement 10 of 11

SHALL Where a run record is listed, the row SHALL lead with that record's status, and the trigger SHALL be shown as a muted qualifier.

WhyA refused firing read `scheduled` next to its own stall reason when the trigger led.

FR-11# Requirement 11 of 11

SHALL A job's card SHALL label its firing count as fired (never runs), and where its recent history holds skipped firings SHALL show their count as refused alongside it; with none, no refused count is shown.

WhyThe fired count counts firings that queued work, so a queue that has only refused reads zero above a list holding a row; naming the refusals reconciles the two.

Design

D1 A firing record becomes `in_progress` only when input is queued, and `failed` at once when the scheduler reports a terminal reason the turn cannot start (scheduler.py `_do_fire_job`).

D2 No periodic sweep: the active-firing flag joins each `in_progress` record to a running run, and startup reconciliation (`run_reconciliation.reconcile_stale_job_runs`) clears what a dead process left. A sweep was rejected as machinery for a residue not observed.

D3 The conversation is created after every refusal point, so only a proceeding firing has one.

D4 `run_count` and `last_run` are stamped where input is queued, once per firing; extra selections of a wide flow write their own run records but do not count.
