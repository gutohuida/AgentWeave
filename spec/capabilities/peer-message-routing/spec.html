Peer message routing

capabilitycurrentsketch

5 MUST·5 requirements·18 criteria

Summary

Which conversation an agent-to-agent message lands in: the forward binding between sender and recipient conversations, the reverse binding for replies, traffic from sources with no conversation, archived threads, and deliberately starting a new thread.

Scope

In scope

  • Binding peer delivery to the sending conversation's lineage
  • Reply (reverse) binding and traffic with no sending conversation
  • Archived recipient threads, by who selected them
  • start_new_thread on the outbound message surface

Non-goals

  • Delivery of the queued entry once routed (inbound-queue-delivery)
  • Archiving conversations (conversation-lifecycle)
  • Rendering peer messages in the transcript (agent-conversation-workspace)

Requirements

FR-1# Requirement 1 of 5

SHALL A peer message that names no recipient conversation SHALL be delivered to the recipient conversation bound to the sending conversation's line of work (the conversation plus every predecessor and successor linked by checkpoint cutover), creating that binding, with `origin: peer`, on first use; delivery MUST NOT be selected by the recipient's recency.

WhySenders do not hold recipient conversation ids, so this rule governs almost all peer traffic; matching the line rather than one id keeps a correspondent on the same thread across a cutover.

FR-2# Requirement 2 of 5

SHALL A message from a source that has no conversation (the Hub or the scheduler) SHALL be delivered to a recipient conversation bound to that source's identity.

WhyOne durable thread per source and recipient leaves no path on which recency routing survives.

FR-3# Requirement 3 of 5

SHALL Delivery to an archived recipient conversation SHALL be refused, with the message content returned, when the sender named it, and SHALL continue into a newly bound successor conversation when the binding resolved it.

WhyA sender that named an archived thread made a correctable error; a thread the operator archived is not the sender's doing.

FR-4# Requirement 4 of 5

SHALL When a peer message names no recipient conversation and no forward binding resolves, delivery SHALL follow the sending conversation's own binding in reverse: if it is bound to a conversation owned by the recipient, the message SHALL be delivered into the newest open conversation of that conversation's line of work rather than into a new conversation.

WhyResolution order is named conversation, forward binding, reverse binding, then creation, so every delivery the forward binding resolves is unchanged and a reply finds the thread it answers.

FR-5# Requirement 5 of 5

SHALL The outbound message surface (`POST /messages`, MCP `send_message`) SHALL accept `start_new_thread`, defaulting to false, which creates a recipient conversation bound to the sending conversation without consulting either binding, so later messages on that line reach it; combining it with a named `conversation_id` SHALL be refused with the content returned.

WhyAn agent needs a way to separate a genuinely new line of work outside a checkpoint cutover; honouring either half of a contradictory request would discard the caller's intent.

Design

D1. Routing is its own capability. Where a message lands is decided once at send time from bindings, independent of how the entry is later delivered or displayed; keeping it apart from delivery keeps each rule set small enough to check against its code.
