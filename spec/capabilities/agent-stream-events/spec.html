Summary

How a run's output becomes a canonical, ordered, safe stream of typed events: normalized from each runner, stored by the Hub, announced only once the write commits, broadcast with a gap notice to subscribers that fall behind, served with the facts of the runs it names, and rendered in the conversation by kind, with Markdown and tool calls formatted safely.

Problem

Each runner reports its work in its own format. Without one contract the Hub would store unsafe or unbounded provider data, the conversation could not tell text from tool activity, and readers would guess a run's facts from whatever events happened to be in a window.

Scope

In scope

  • Normalization of Claude, Codex (exec and app-server) and Copilot (ACP) output into canonical events
  • Event kinds, payload versioning, tool-call correlation, run identity and ordering
  • Redaction, run-secret scrubbing and payload bounds
  • Output persistence, the output routes, the agent_output SSE event and legacy compatibility
  • Announcing a write only after it commits, and the `stream_gap` frame for a subscriber that fell behind
  • Run facts served beside timeline and chat entries, and the persisted closing status row
  • How the conversation renders entries: Markdown, tool-call formatting, work blocks and execution order

Non-goals

  • Context-window usage (agent-context-usage) and token/credit accounting (usage-accounting)
  • How a turn's terminal outcome is shown and refreshes its conversation: turn-outcome-visibility
  • Conversation layout, folding and following: agent-conversation-workspace; the composer: agent-composer

Requirements

FR-20# Requirement 1 of 31

SHALL The Hub SHALL normalize every supported runner's output into a provider-neutral observation carrying zero or more ordered output events, and, separately, an optional context-usage sample, an optional accounting sample and an optional provider session id.

WhyOne envelope lets persistence, transport and rendering ignore which runner produced the output, and keeps usage and accounting from being disguised as display events.

FR-21# Requirement 2 of 31

SHALL Every output event SHALL have exactly one kind from `text`, `thinking`, `tool_use`, `tool_result`, `status`, `diagnostic` or `error`; the Hub SHALL refuse any other kind on ingress.

WhyA closed set is what lets every consumer render by kind without guessing from content.

FR-22# Requirement 3 of 31

SHALL Every structured payload SHALL carry `version: 1` and the fields its kind defines.

WhyThe version lets the payload shape evolve without breaking stored rows or older clients.

FR-23# Requirement 4 of 31

SHALL Tool-use and tool-result events SHALL carry the provider's call identifier as `call_id` whenever the provider supplies one, together with a readable tool name and summary, and SHALL NOT invent one when the provider gives none.

WhyPairing a call with its result is how the conversation shows whether a call completed or failed.

FR-24# Requirement 5 of 31

SHALL Every run the Hub starts SHALL receive a unique `run_id`, and every event recorded for that run SHALL carry a `sequence` that increases strictly within the run, assigned after normalization; the run's closing status row takes the next sequence after its last event.

WhyTimestamps can tie; run identity plus sequence gives every reader one deterministic order.

FR-25# Requirement 6 of 31

SHALL The Hub SHALL normalize into the canonical event contract the output of each runner it can start: Claude Code's `stream-json` lines, Codex's `exec` JSON lines and its app-server items, and Copilot's ACP session updates.

WhyThese are the only transports the runner adapters offer; each must produce the same timeline shape.

FR-26# Requirement 7 of 31

SHALL Runner adapters SHALL tolerate provider events they do not recognize without ending the run's stream: an unrecognized structured event SHALL be omitted or reported as a bounded `diagnostic`, and a line that is not structured at all SHALL be kept as readable text.

WhyProviders add event types without notice; a new one must not cost the operator the rest of a turn.

FR-27# Requirement 8 of 31

SHALL Before an event is stored or broadcast, the Hub SHALL redact known secret patterns and secret-named fields, SHALL remove every value registered as secret for that run, SHALL bound a tool result to 8 KiB and any payload to 64 KiB (marking `truncated`), and SHALL NOT copy raw provider events or opaque or encrypted reasoning fields.

WhyTool inputs and outputs routinely carry credentials and large blobs; the stream is stored and broadcast, so it must be safe and bounded at the point it is written.

FR-28# Requirement 9 of 31

SHALL Agent output storage, `POST /agents/{name}/output`, `GET /agents/{name}/output` and the `agent_output` SSE event SHALL carry nullable `kind`, `payload`, `run_id` and `sequence` beside content, session, conversation and timestamp, and SHALL reject a request whose kind is unknown or whose serialized payload exceeds 64 KiB.

WhyThe structured fields were added to an existing text-only contract, so they must be optional, and ingress must refuse what the renderer cannot trust.

FR-29# Requirement 10 of 31

SHALL The Hub and its UI SHALL accept and present output rows with no structured fields as plain text, and every structured event SHALL keep meaningful `content` as a plain-text fallback.

WhyOld rows and self-reporting producers predate the structured contract and must still read correctly.

FR-30# Requirement 11 of 31

SHALL Chat history responses SHALL carry, for each agent-output entry, its stream kind, payload, run id, sequence and timestamp rather than its content alone.

WhyThe conversation renders by kind and groups by run; flattening to content would lose both.

FR-31# Requirement 12 of 31

SHALL `GET /agents/{name}/output` without a cursor SHALL return the newest N rows in chronological order; with a `since` timestamp it SHALL return up to N rows after that time, oldest first; ties SHALL be broken by sequence and then by row id.

WhyA bounded window must show the latest activity, and a cursor must resume without gaps or repeats.

FR-32# Requirement 13 of 31

SHALL The conversation view SHALL classify each agent-output entry by its structured `kind` (message, work or result) and SHALL NOT inspect content prefixes to decide how an entry renders; an entry with no kind renders as message text.

WhyClassifying by kind is what makes every runner's output render the same way.

FR-33# Requirement 14 of 31

SHALL Thinking events SHALL be presented as work, grouped with adjacent tool activity into a work block that is collapsed until the operator expands it, rather than as conversational text.

WhyReasoning is useful on demand but would otherwise bury the answer the operator is reading.

FR-34# Requirement 15 of 31

SHALL A tool-use event SHALL render together with the tool result that shares its `call_id` in the same work block, showing whether the call completed, failed or is awaiting a result; diagnostics SHALL be visually distinct from errors and from agent text.

WhyThe operator needs to see what a call did and whether it failed without reading two rows.

FR-35# Requirement 16 of 31

SHALL The repository SHALL include fixtures and tests for each runner transport's normalization, for output persistence and SSE delivery, legacy rows, payload bounds and redaction, ordering, and the conversation's rendering of structured entries.

WhyEach adapter maps a provider format the Hub does not control; fixtures are what catch drift.

FR-37# Requirement 17 of 31

MUST The entries of a turn SHALL be presented in the order they occurred, with consecutive work entries collapsed into one work block; tool activity MUST NOT be moved ahead of text that preceded it.

WhyReordering misrepresents what the agent did before it said what it said.

FR-38# Requirement 18 of 31

MUST Every work block in a turn SHALL carry its own expansion state and a duration spanning its own first and last entry; expanding one block MUST NOT expand another.

WhyBlocks are separate stretches of work; one shared toggle or duration would misreport them.

FR-40# Requirement 19 of 31

SHALL Every response that serves run facts (the agent timeline, a conversation's chat history and an agent's recent chat) SHALL carry, keyed by `run_id`, each run's status (with `running` reported as `started`), exit code, start and end time and its recorded error (bounded, or null where it recorded none), read from the run's own row; clients SHALL NOT reconstruct them from event names or times.

WhyThe run row is the record of how a run ended; lifecycle events in a window can be missing or late.

FR-41# Requirement 20 of 31

SHALL The run-facts map of each such response SHALL cover every run named by an entry the response itself returns, after any truncation, by looking those runs up by id within the response's project, and SHALL omit a run that has no row.

WhyAny window, limit or ordering-based query drops old runs that new events name, leaving turns with no outcome.

FR-42# Requirement 21 of 31

SHALL The Hub SHALL persist a run's closing status row (`Run <status> (exit <code>).`, kind `status`, payload `phase: completed` and the exit code) on both the process and app-server paths; where the write fails it SHALL log the failure naming the run and leave the run's recorded outcome unchanged.

WhySome runners emit no completion sentinel of their own, so this row is the only durable end marker in the stream.

FR-44# Requirement 22 of 31

SHALL A UI model function that consumes an API payload SHALL be tested against a fixture in the order that route actually returns, and some test SHALL fail if that order is reversed.

WhyA fixture in an order the route never emits can pass while the behaviour cannot fire in production.

FR-47# Requirement 23 of 31

SHALL Every event the Hub persists or broadcasts SHALL use `run_id` only for the id of a run; an event or route answer that refers to a job's firing record SHALL name it `job_run_id`.

WhyConsumers look `run_id` up as a run; a firing-record id under that key resolves to nothing.

FR-48# Requirement 24 of 31

SHALL The Hub SHALL NOT turn the history Copilot replays when a session is loaded into new events, and SHALL begin mapping a Copilot turn's session updates only once that turn's prompt has been sent.

WhyReplayed history would duplicate earlier turns inside the new one.

FR-49# Requirement 25 of 31

SHALL The Hub SHALL remove a value registered as secret for a run from that run's recorded `text` and `thinking` events when the value runs across two or more of them, as well as when it lies inside one.

WhyProviders stream text in arbitrary chunks, so a per-event scrub alone can leak a split secret.

FR-50# Requirement 26 of 31

SHALL The Hub SHALL record in a Copilot run's stream each root compaction, each error and each subagent start, completion and failure that Copilot reports, as structured events carrying the facts Copilot reported, and SHALL record an error reported both structurally and as text only once.

WhyThese change what the conversation holds and why a turn failed; the operator must see them.

FR-51# Requirement 27 of 31

SHALL A streamed output row that cannot be written because the database is locked SHALL be retried and then dropped with a warning naming the run, and SHALL NOT fail the run.

WhyA lost timeline row is a smaller harm than marking a run failed while its agent works on unobserved.

FR-52# Requirement 28 of 31

SHALL Operator input, agent text output and peer traffic SHALL be rendered as GitHub-flavoured Markdown with single newlines rendered as line breaks; the renderer MUST NOT interpret raw HTML, and text with no Markdown syntax SHALL render as plain text.

WhyConversation content includes text the operator did not author, so raw HTML is never markup.

FR-53# Requirement 29 of 31

SHALL A tool-call entry SHALL be presented with an icon and label from a fixed mapping keyed on the tool's name, falling back to a generic icon and label for unknown names; where its recorded input carries both an old and a new string and was not truncated, its expanded view SHALL render the change as a diff, otherwise the raw input and output.

WhyA diff of truncated content would misrepresent the change.

FR-54# Requirement 30 of 31

MUST The Hub SHALL broadcast an event reporting a database write only after the transaction holding that write commits, MUST NOT broadcast it when the transaction rolls back, and a failure to publish a deferred announcement MUST NOT fail the commit or change the route's answer.

WhyA client that refetches on an event must find the change the event described.

FR-55# Requirement 31 of 31

SHALL When the Hub drops events for a subscriber whose queue is full, that stream SHALL send one `stream_gap` frame with the number dropped and no `project_id` as soon as it is sending again, and the app SHALL refresh all server state as on a reconnect and show in Activity that events were not delivered.

WhyDropping for a slow reader keeps other broadcasters moving, but a silent drop leaves the app showing stale state as current.

Design

D1. Normalize in the Hub. Adapters emit RunEvents (kind, content, versioned payload); the Hub re-implements the CLI's taxonomy rather than importing it, because the Hub does not depend on agentweave-ai. Stream transports return a ParsedLine per line; RPC transports (Codex app-server, Copilot ACP) return events directly. D2. Safety at the write. record_agent_output is the single writer for self-reported and Hub-spawned output: it scrubs the run's registered secrets from content and payload, stores the row and broadcasts agent_output. Values split across text/thinking events are joined and scrubbed at the executors (run_secrets.scrub_stream), not in the writer, because the writer is retried on lock. D3. Observational writes never fail a run. Streamed rows and the closing status row go through _record_observation: a lock is retried, then dropped with a warning naming the run. D4. Run facts come from the run row, looked up by the ids the response returns, after truncation, scoped to the project, with no ORDER BY or LIMIT. Status `running` is renamed `started` at the boundary; the error is truncated, not redacted, to match the run_failed broadcast. D5. The closing status row (`phase: completed`, exit code) is the stream's durable end marker for every runner, since Codex and Copilot emit no completion sentinel. D6. The conversation groups entries into turns by run_id and into blocks in execution order; consecutive thinking/tool entries form one collapsed work block with its own state and duration. D7. Announce after commit. A write's event is staged with the transaction and published from the session's `after_commit` hook; a full subscriber queue counts its drops and the stream reports them once as `stream_gap`.
