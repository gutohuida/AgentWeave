10 MUST·10 requirements·26 criteria

Summary

How the Hub answers the pointer and the keyboard: every activatable element shows resting, hover, pressed and focus states from shared tokens without moving anything; hover-revealed row actions keep their space; one Escape dismisses one thing; and menus and dialogs put the keyboard where the operator's next input belongs.

Problem

Elements that did not respond to the pointer looked broken, emphasis that shifted layout made lists jitter, and keyboard focus left behind a dialog or on a menu trigger made the operator's keystrokes act on something they could not see.

Scope

In scope

  • Resting, hover, pressed, selected and focus treatments of activatable elements
  • Hover-revealed row actions
  • Motion and reduced motion for state changes
  • Escape ownership between nested surfaces
  • Focus placement for menus and modal dialogs

Non-goals

  • Shell layout, palette and the contrast ramp (hub-workspace-shell)
  • Composer-specific control treatment (agent-composer)

Requirements

FR-7# Requirement 1 of 10

SHALL Every activatable element (controls, navigation and list rows, tabs, cards, disclosures) SHALL present distinct resting, hover and pressed states and a visible keyboard focus indicator; the shared Button primitive and row treatment SHALL supply these so no element that changes state when clicked is inert under the pointer.

WhyAn element that does not answer the pointer reads as broken or decorative.

FR-8# Requirement 2 of 10

MUST A selected or current element SHALL look different from a merely hovered one and SHALL remain identifiable while the pointer is elsewhere; the hover, pressed and selected treatments MUST NOT resolve to the same appearance.

WhyWhere the operator is and where the pointer is are different facts.

FR-9# Requirement 3 of 10

MUST Changing between resting, hover, pressed and selected states MUST NOT change an element's size or position or displace any neighbour; controls SHALL reserve their border space at rest.

WhyA one-pixel shift on hover makes a list jitter under the pointer.

FR-10# Requirement 4 of 10

MUST A row's secondary actions that appear only on hover or focus SHALL occupy their layout space at rest (revealed by opacity), SHALL appear when the row has keyboard focus, SHALL stay visible while their menu is open or for the active item, and MUST remain reachable by keyboard.

WhyRevealing an action must not move the row, and an action that only the mouse can find does not exist for the keyboard.

FR-11# Requirement 5 of 10

SHALL Transitions between interaction states SHALL use the shared duration and easing tokens; when the operator prefers reduced motion, transitions and animations SHALL be reduced to effectively instant while every state stays distinguishable by appearance alone.

WhyEasing makes state changes legible; motion must never be the only signal.

FR-12# Requirement 6 of 10

MUST Hover, pressed and selected treatments SHALL resolve from semantic tokens defined in both modes (`--row-hover`, `--row-active`, `--row-selected` for rows), and the same kind of element SHALL use the same tokens wherever it appears; a component MUST NOT hard-code a colour for an interaction state.

WhyOne ladder of tokens keeps the three states from collapsing onto each other on some surfaces.

FR-13# Requirement 7 of 10

SHALL An Escape keystroke SHALL be answered by exactly one surface, the innermost one that owns it: a nested control that handles Escape SHALL mark the event handled (`preventDefault`), and a containing surface SHALL NOT dismiss on an Escape already handled. Of several stacked dialogs, only the newest SHALL answer.

WhyA panel that closes along with the menu or field inside it takes the operator's context with it, and the nested control can never be seen to work. The containing listener sits on `document` in the bubble phase so every nearer handler has already run.

FR-14# Requirement 8 of 10

SHALL A menu action that presents a control expecting input SHALL be declared as taking focus and SHALL leave keyboard focus in that control once the menu closes, running after the menu has gone; every other menu action SHALL return focus to the menu's trigger.

WhyReturning focus to the trigger after an action that asks a question sends the operator's typing to a button; nothing errors and nothing appears.

FR-15# Requirement 9 of 10

SHALL Every modal dialog SHALL record where focus was, move focus into itself on open (to the control marked as initial focus, else its first enabled control, else the panel itself), and return focus to the recorded element on close. A confirmation of an action that cannot be undone SHALL mark its non-destructive choice as initial focus. While a dialog waits on an action that disabled the focused control, the dialog panel SHALL hold focus.

WhyFocus left behind a dialog answers the next key with the control that opened it, repeating the action being asked about; marking the initial control explicitly keeps a restyle from moving the keyboard onto a destructive choice.

FR-16# Requirement 10 of 10

MUST While a modal dialog is open, Tab and Shift+Tab SHALL cycle through its enabled controls and MUST NOT move focus behind it, including on the first Tab when focus is still outside the panel; only the newest of stacked dialogs SHALL handle Tab.

WhyA first Tab that fell through to the page landed behind the scrim, where the operator cannot see what they are operating.

Design

D1. States come from primitives, not per-screen styles: the Button primitive carries a permanent transparent border and a focus-visible ring; every list row uses the shared `.row-item` treatment and the `--row-hover` / `--row-active` / `--row-selected` ladder.

D2. Escape ownership is signalled on the event itself (`defaultPrevented`); `useDialogFocus` listens on `document` in the bubble phase so it is always the last to see the key, and keeps a stack so only the newest dialog answers.

D3. Menu items declare `takesFocus` when they present an input; such an item runs as the menu closes and the menu does not restore focus to its trigger.

D4. Dialog initial focus is marked explicitly (`data-dialog-initial-focus`) rather than inferred from DOM order.
