Inbound queue delivery

capabilitycurrentsketch

27 MUST·27 requirements·99 criteria

Summary

How input queued for an agent becomes a turn: delivery order, one kind of entry per turn, the hop budget at delivery, retries and give-up, refusals reported to the input they concern, held entries, and withdrawal.

Scope

In scope

  • Delivering queued input intact and in order, one kind (review or work) per turn
  • Counting attempts, retrying, starting a fresh provider session and giving up
  • Refusals and starts reported only to the input they concern; queue-status reasons
  • Hop-budget holds at delivery, release and withdrawal of queue entries

Non-goals

  • Which conversation a peer message is bound to (peer-message-routing)
  • Holds caused by a provider's usage allowance (provider-allowance-holds)
  • How the queue is shown in the transcript (agent-conversation-workspace)

Requirements

FR-1# Requirement 1 of 27

MUST Every queued input the Hub marks as delivered SHALL reach the agent's model in full, including content spanning several lines; the Hub MUST NOT report an input as delivered when the launch path cannot carry it, and the only change delivery makes is neutralising file mentions in text the operator did not write.

WhyOn Windows an npm `.cmd` shim is executed by cmd.exe, which truncates an argument at its first newline; the Hub unwraps such a shim to the executable it forwards to.

FR-2# Requirement 2 of 27

SHALL The system SHALL return a failed run's input to the queue however the run failed, keeping its place in arrival order and its conversation, except for a run the operator stopped and a run failed for reporting a provider session other than the conversation's binding; returning input SHALL trigger another delivery attempt without operator action.

WhyA runtime that dies mid-turn is the likeliest failure; a stopped turn and a binding-conflict turn already did what they were going to do, so re-delivering them would repeat work.

FR-3# Requirement 3 of 27

SHALL Input delivered after an earlier delivery failed or was refused by the provider's allowance SHALL state, per entry, which attempt this is and that an earlier attempt was cut off before it finished; first deliveries SHALL carry no such note.

WhyThe agent cannot otherwise tell that half-finished work in its checkout is its own; what to do about it is left to the agent.

FR-4# Requirement 4 of 27

SHALL An inbound queue entry whose hop depth exceeds the project's hop budget SHALL NOT be delivered in any turn, and a turn's depth SHALL be the depth of the entry that admitted it, never the lowest depth among the entries it carries.

WhyTaking the minimum let a shallow entry carry a deep one and restart the chain's count.

FR-5# Requirement 5 of 27

SHALL The Hub SHALL report an entry queued because its hop depth exceeds the budget as held for that reason, and the operator SHALL be able to release it — re-basing its depth to 0, recording the release with the depth it was released from, and scheduling the agent — or discard it.

WhyA bound with no exit is a wedge; re-basing to 0 records "the operator restarted this chain here".

FR-6# Requirement 6 of 27

SHALL Where an agent's queue holds both a review entry and a work entry in one conversation, a turn SHALL carry only the kind of the entry that admitted it (entries naming neither ride with either), and the other kind SHALL stay queued for a later turn.

WhyA turn has one workspace, so a mixed batch delivered a turn that was neither.

FR-7# Requirement 7 of 27

SHALL The system SHALL refuse, before any workspace is prepared or process started, a turn whose input asks for both a review and work on a task, or for reviews of more than one task, naming the tasks and stating that a turn has one subject.

WhyDefence in depth for a caller that hands `queue_entry_ids` to the trigger directly, bypassing the scheduler's narrowing.

FR-8# Requirement 8 of 27

SHALL A request submitting input to an agent SHALL be answered as a failure, with the refused condition's own status code and sentence, where the input cannot be delivered because of what the request asked for; input that waits on the environment (no runner bound, CLI missing, workspace not preparable), a turn in flight, or a budget or hold that will clear SHALL be answered as accepted and queued.

WhyAnswering a refusal as "queued" tells the operator to wait for something that will never happen; answering an environment wait as a failure would discard input the repair is meant to deliver.

FR-9# Requirement 9 of 27

SHALL Where the system refuses a turn, it SHALL report that refusal only to a request whose own input the refused turn would have carried, and SHALL tell any other request that its input is queued behind other input.

WhyThe turn is built from the oldest eligible input, which may belong to a conversation the caller cannot see.

FR-10# Requirement 10 of 27

SHALL Where a request naming one conversation (the trigger or `POST /conversations/{id}/continue`) causes a turn to start, it SHALL be answered as started only if the turn carried that conversation's input; otherwise it SHALL identify the conversation that did start and say whether the named conversation's input is waiting behind other input, was given up, or had nothing queued.

WhyA start reported to the wrong conversation is indistinguishable from success; input selection itself stays in arrival order.

FR-11# Requirement 11 of 27

SHALL The Continue control on a conversation with queued work and no run SHALL distinguish a turn that began in this conversation from one that began elsewhere, naming the other conversation by its title, and SHALL state the server's reason when nothing started.

WhyRendering one confirmation for both outcomes invites a second press that starts a turn nobody asked for.

FR-12# Requirement 12 of 27

SHALL Input whose submitting request was answered with a refusal about what it asked for SHALL be withdrawn from the queue with the refusal as its reason and announced as withdrawn (not as given up after attempts); input waiting on the environment SHALL stay queued.

WhyThe operator was told synchronously that the request failed; a queue still counting the input would say the opposite.

FR-13# Requirement 13 of 27

SHALL Where a submission is refused, the interface SHALL present the refusal's own sentence to the operator.

WhyA correct status that reaches no readable message has not told the operator anything.

FR-14# Requirement 14 of 27

SHALL The system SHALL NOT count a delivery attempt against queued input where the turn was refused for a reason that prevents the agent from running at all, or where the agent's own workspace cannot be prepared and no other queued input would have run in a different workspace; every other refusal of a turn SHALL count an attempt.

WhyCounting the operator's own retries against an agent that cannot run destroys input nothing tried to deliver; where other input could run, the head of the queue really is in the way. When uncertain, the system holds rather than counts.

FR-15# Requirement 15 of 27

SHALL The system SHALL re-evaluate every agent holding queued input in a project whenever a run in that project reaches a terminal status, on every execution path, regardless of whether the bookkeeping after the terminal status succeeds, and a later bookkeeping failure MUST NOT relabel a run that had already ended.

WhyNothing re-evaluates the queue on a timer, and restart reconciliation only examines runs still recorded as running, so a release gated on bookkeeping strands input indefinitely.

FR-16# Requirement 16 of 27

SHALL The system SHALL bring every run whose execution ends inside the process that started it to a terminal status on every execution path without a restart, recording and logging why when it ended abnormally, while still propagating deliberate cancellation.

WhyAn agent with a run stuck in `running` is refused every later turn; a run whose Hub was killed is left to startup reconciliation.

FR-17# Requirement 17 of 27

SHALL Where an attempt to start an agent's turn ends by giving up on queued input, the system SHALL, in the same pass, attempt the input queued behind it, SHALL continue only after a give-up, SHALL count an attempt against any one input at most once per pass, and SHALL answer the request from the attempt that ended the pass (the give-up's refusal where the queue was then empty).

WhyGiving up releases the queue only if something then tries it; counting a rider each time would spend its allowance in one pass.

FR-18# Requirement 18 of 27

SHALL Where an agent's queue status can find no present reason for waiting input and falls back to the refusal of the last delivery attempt, it SHALL present it as "the last delivery attempt was refused: …", except that a refusal naming another agent's turn as holding a task checkout SHALL be checked now and named as the present reason only while that turn still runs; a failed check SHALL still yield an answer.

WhyA remembered refusal may have stopped being true; presenting it as current contradicts the live state beside it.

FR-19# Requirement 19 of 27

SHALL Where the Hub gave up delivering a message, the conversation SHALL show the message as not delivered with the Hub's reason and, where a run was the last attempt, how that run ended and the error it recorded; it SHALL learn of the give-up on every path, without a reload.

WhyA run that failed before its process started writes no output, so without this the reason sits only on the run's row.

FR-20# Requirement 20 of 27

SHALL When a peer message is queued at a hop depth beyond the project's budget, the Hub SHALL record a `queue_chain_suspended` event and SHALL tell the sender, in the send's response, that the message is held by the hop budget.

WhyA sender told only that its message was sent waits for a reply that the budget will never let happen.

FR-21# Requirement 21 of 27

SHALL Withdrawing or releasing a queue entry that is not queued SHALL be refused with a reason naming its actual state — unknown in this project (404), already delivered (naming the run), given up by the Hub (with its reason), or already withdrawn (409) — and releasing an entry within the hop budget SHALL be refused because the budget is not what holds it.

WhyOne sentence for every state told an operator whose entry had been given up that it was delivered.

FR-22# Requirement 22 of 27

SHALL Delivering, withdrawing, counting and giving up on a queue entry SHALL each take effect only if the entry is still queued at the moment of the write, so a withdrawal and a delivery or give-up racing for one entry cannot both succeed.

WhyA read-then-write let a delivery overwrite a withdrawal the operator had already been answered 200 for.

FR-23# Requirement 23 of 27

SHALL `POST /agent/trigger` SHALL refuse before queueing anything a request it can already tell will never run: an invalid agent name (400), an archived agent (409), a conversation that is unknown, another agent's or archived (409), a `work_dir` overriding a writing agent's isolation (400), a task already decided (409), a review the task cannot receive (409/403), and runtime overrides with no runner bound (409) or that the catalog rejects (400).

WhyThe operator is looking at the response; a refusal at spawn would only surface later as a failed or silently unbound run.

FR-24# Requirement 24 of 27

SHALL `GET /queue/{agent}/status` SHALL explain waiting input with the first reason that applies, in this order: the agent is running; every entry is over the hop budget; a provider allowance hold; the token budget is spent for autonomous input; the agent is not launchable; the project workspace is unavailable; another agent's running turn holds the task checkout; the last attempt's recorded refusal; and finally the failed-attempt count with attempts left.

WhyEach live check describes the agent now; the stored refusal and the attempt count are records of the past and only explain the wait when nothing current does.

FR-25# Requirement 25 of 27

SHALL After an input's second failed delivery, the system SHALL clear its conversation's provider session so the next delivery starts a fresh provider session.

WhyOne failure is routinely a restart or a transient spawn error and must not discard provider-side context; repeated failure suggests the session itself cannot be resumed.

FR-26# Requirement 26 of 27

SHALL After an input's third failed delivery or counted refusal, the system SHALL stop attempting it, withdraw it with a recorded reason, keep the run that last carried it, and report the give-up to the operator as `queue_entry_abandoned`.

WhyOne attempt on the old session, one that trips the reset, one on a fresh session; more would wedge an agent across further failing turns before anyone is told.

FR-27# Requirement 27 of 27

SHALL Where a Copilot turn fails on a root `session.error` whose `errorType` is `authentication` or `quota` and no allowance refusal with a reset ahead applies, the system SHALL count the attempt, withdraw the input at once with a reason naming the failure and stating the Hub did not retry, and keep the conversation's provider session.

WhyA refused credential or spent quota fails the same way on every retry, so each retry only spends a run and an error card.

Design

D1. Delivery is its own capability. The queue's contract (attempt counting, give-up, refusal attribution, withdrawal races) is backend behaviour with its own tests; it was a third of agent-conversation-workspace while having nothing to do with how a conversation looks.

D2. Allowance holds are split out to provider-allowance-holds: they pause the queue for a reason outside the Hub and carry their own notices; everything else that decides whether and when an entry is delivered stays here.
