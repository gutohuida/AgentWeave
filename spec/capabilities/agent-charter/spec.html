Agent charter

capabilitycurrentsketch

9 MUST·9 requirements·14 criteria

Summary

Charters are project-scoped, operator-authored markdown records describing what an agent is answerable for. Each project is seeded once with a bundled starter set. An agent binds to at most one charter, whose content is injected verbatim into the agent's turn context. A charter is managed on the Charters page and cannot be deleted while an open agent holds it.

Problem

Behaviour defined by a fixed list of personas, or by files on disk, cannot be edited by the operator and drifts from what the runtime actually offers; a charter that describes an activity rather than an accountability reads as a topic label and guarantees nothing.

Scope

In scope

  • The `charters` table and `/api/v1/charters` routes.
  • The bundled starter set in `hub/hub/data/charters/` and its one-time seeding.
  • The Charters page and the charter binding on an agent.

Non-goals

  • How the charter is placed among the rest of an agent's context (agent-context-onboarding).
  • Releasing a charter when its agent is archived (agent-configuration).

Requirements

FR-8# Requirement 1 of 9

SHALL The Hub SHALL persist each charter as a project-scoped database row with a non-blank name and markdown content, created, read, edited and deleted through `/api/v1/charters`, and SHALL NOT represent charters as files on disk or a hard-coded persona list.

WhyA charter is the operator's to write and change; an edit must reach the next turn without a release.

FR-9# Requirement 2 of 9

SHALL The Hub SHALL seed each project with the bundled starter charter set exactly once (on registration, or at startup for a project not yet seeded), recording that it did so, and SHALL NOT seed again, add, remove or reconcile charters afterwards, even if the operator deletes every charter or the bundled set changes.

WhyAfter seeding the charters are the operator's; reconciling them with a newer bundle would overwrite their edits.

FR-10# Requirement 3 of 9

SHALL Each agent SHALL reference at most one charter; a bound charter's content SHALL be injected verbatim into the agent's context, and an agent with no charter SHALL remain usable, its context stating "No charter is assigned to this agent." and listing `charter` as missing.

WhyA charter is optional guidance; its absence must be said plainly rather than fabricated or treated as an error.

FR-11# Requirement 4 of 9

SHALL The Hub UI SHALL provide a Charters page to list, read, create, edit and delete charters, where each charter starts collapsed to a summary, its full content can be expanded read-only without opening an editor, and several can be expanded at once; an agent's charter SHALL be bound from the agent's configuration.

WhyA charter's content is the text injected into a turn, so an operator choosing one must be able to read and compare it in full without risking an edit.

FR-16# Requirement 5 of 9

SHALL Deleting a charter from the Charters page SHALL first ask for confirmation, naming the charter and how much content would be lost.

WhyCharter deletion is a hard delete with no copy kept; rows can share a name, so the size is what tells two apart.

FR-15# Requirement 6 of 9

SHALL Deleting a charter an agent holds SHALL be refused with 409 naming every holder and saying to unbind before deleting, and a charter no agent holds SHALL be deleted; because archival releases an agent's charter, every holder named SHALL be an open agent the default roster shows.

WhyThe repair (unbind, then delete) works only if the operator can find the named agent; the guarantee rests on archived agents holding no charter, not on a filter in this route.

FR-12# Requirement 7 of 9

SHALL A seeded charter SHALL describe only mechanisms the runtime offers: it SHALL NOT tell an agent to read a file the system does not create, run a command that does not exist, address a charter title or a participant not guaranteed to exist, restate a rule the system enforces in code, or gather orientation the turn already supplies; escalation and ambiguity SHALL go to the operator.

WhyCharter content is injected verbatim, so it is instruction, not documentation; a roster is whatever the operator created, and the operator is the one participant always reachable.

FR-13# Requirement 8 of 9

SHALL A seeded charter SHALL state what its holder is answerable for, not an activity, technology or phase; charters answerable for the same outcome SHALL be one charter with an operator-filled scope; and no seeded charter SHALL take on coordination the system performs in code.

WhyA charter asking a model to guarantee in prose what a service guarantees in code is an unenforced second authority.

FR-14# Requirement 9 of 9

SHALL The starter set SHALL include at least one pair of charters from a non-software domain (today Underwriter and Underwriting Approver) whose separation is a control, each stating which step belongs to the other holder and that it may not perform it.

WhyIn software one capable model can do every step, which makes charters look like topic labels; a real separation of duties shows a charter carrying a guarantee.

Design

**Records, not files.** `Charter` rows replaced the fixed role-guide list (`runner-agent-charter-separation`, 2026-08-03). The bundled Markdown files are only a seed.

**Seed once, by flag.** `Project.charters_seeded` makes seeding a one-time event per project, so deleting every charter does not bring the bundle back.

**Starter set of nine.** Tech Lead, Developer, Code Reviewer, Verifier, Security Engineer, Guardian, Spec Author, and the Underwriter / Underwriting Approver pair, curated in 2026-08 from 21 role guides against the accountability test.

**Deletion relies on archival.** The delete route queries holders without a lifecycle filter; that is correct only because archival clears `charter_id` (and migration 0105 cleared older archived agents).
