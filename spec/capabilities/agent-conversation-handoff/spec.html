Agent conversation handoff

capabilitycurrentsketch

10 MUST·10 requirements·31 criteria

Summary

The operator hands a conversation over to a successor: the Hub writes a checkpoint of the conversation, then a cutover opens one successor conversation in the same line of work, queues the checkpoint into it, and archives the predecessor. A conversation is handed over at most once, and the successor can start from its checkpoint without the operator typing anything.

Problem

A long conversation fills its context window. Continuing it means either letting the provider compact it with a summary nobody checked, or starting fresh and losing the thread. Asking the agent to write its own handoff file proved unreliable: files landed in the wrong place or were never written while the run reported success.

Scope

In scope

  • The conversation header's Checkpoint (handoff) control and the transition notices around it
  • The cutover route: successor creation, checkpoint delivery, predecessor archive, refusals
  • Line-of-work continuity across a cutover
  • At-most-once handover, enforced by the database
  • Starting a successor from its queued checkpoint

Non-goals

  • How a checkpoint is generated, verified, cited and when it is due (conversation-checkpoint)
  • Peer delivery routing by line of work (agent-conversation-workspace)
  • Archive and unarchive in general (conversation-lifecycle)

Requirements

FR-5# Requirement 1 of 10

MUST The open conversation's header SHALL show a labelled Checkpoint control at rest, not behind a menu. When it cannot be used it SHALL stay visible, disabled, stating why: the agent's runner is manual, no conversation is open, a checkpoint is already being written, or the agent is busy. The header MUST NOT offer legacy Compact or Reset actions, and conversations SHALL be identified by AgentWeave `conversation_id`, never by provider session id.

WhyOperators forget a handoff exists if it is hidden; a disabled control that says why keeps the header from shifting between agents.

FR-6# Requirement 2 of 10

SHALL Activating Checkpoint SHALL ask the Hub to generate a checkpoint of the open conversation on the project's configured checkpoint runner (no agent turn is involved), and only when that checkpoint's status is `ready` SHALL it then request the cutover. If the checkpoint is `unwritten` or `failed`, nothing SHALL be cut over and the operator SHALL be told why.

WhyReadiness is a property of a verified checkpoint record, not of a run having ended; the earlier design reported success for runs that produced nothing.

FR-11# Requirement 3 of 10

SHALL `POST /api/v1/projects/{project_id}/conversations/{conversation_id}/checkpoint` SHALL answer 409 when the project has no checkpoint runner configured, and 409 when a checkpoint is already being written for that conversation; the UI SHALL coalesce repeated presses for one conversation into one request.

WhyThe Hub will not spend another agent's runner on the operator's behalf, and two concurrent generations for one conversation would bill twice for one handoff.

FR-7# Requirement 4 of 10

MUST `POST /api/v1/projects/{project_id}/checkpoints/{checkpoint_id}/cutover` SHALL, for a `ready` checkpoint, create exactly one successor conversation (origin `handoff`, title `Continued: <predecessor title>` without stacking the prefix, recorded as operator-set, inheriting the predecessor's runtime overrides and peer binding), queue the rendered checkpoint into the successor as conversation-scoped input with a preamble saying where it came from, archive the predecessor, broadcast `conversation_cut_over`, and return the successor's id. The checkpoint MUST NOT be delivered through agent-scoped context, and no message SHALL instruct the agent to find a checkpoint on disk.

WhyThe successor and its queued checkpoint are created before the predecessor is archived, so a failure part-way leaves a usable predecessor; agent-scoped context is one file per agent and cannot carry one conversation's checkpoint.

FR-12# Requirement 5 of 10

SHALL A cutover SHALL be refused with 409 and a stated reason, leaving no successor and no queued entry, when the checkpoint is not `ready`, when the predecessor has a run that has not ended, or when it has undelivered queued input.

WhyCutting over anyway would leave a successor holding a summary while the predecessor holds the actual work.

FR-8# Requirement 6 of 10

SHALL The conversation surface SHALL state which transition applies: continuing the open conversation (named by its title), writing a checkpoint, continuing in the successor after a cutover, or starting a fresh conversation when none is open. On a successful cutover the view SHALL move to the successor immediately. Handoff and notice state SHALL be cleared when the operator moves to another agent or conversation.

WhyThe operator should always know which conversation the next message goes to; state left over from another conversation would describe the wrong one.

FR-13# Requirement 7 of 10

SHALL A conversation holding queued input and no running turn SHALL offer a Continue control that starts its queued work without a new message, via `POST /api/v1/projects/{project_id}/conversations/{conversation_id}/continue`; when the project's `checkpoint_auto_continue` setting is on, a cutover SHALL itself try to start the successor. The operator's next message in a successor SHALL be sent as typed, with no resume prefix.

WhyThe successor already has everything it needs; requiring the operator to invent a sentence to get it moving made the handover a dead end.

FR-14# Requirement 8 of 10

SHALL When the Hub has generated a `ready` checkpoint for the open conversation because of context pressure, the conversation SHALL show it as an offer in its banner stack, with an action that cuts over to a successor from that checkpoint; an `unwritten` or `failed` checkpoint SHALL NOT be offered.

WhyA checkpoint written and stored but never surfaced looked to the operator as if checkpointing did not work.

FR-9# Requirement 9 of 10

SHALL A successor opened by cutover SHALL carry its predecessor's `lineage_id`, recorded on the conversation itself; every other conversation SHALL be created with a `lineage_id` equal to its own id. Lineage SHALL be resolvable without reading checkpoint records.

WhyPeer delivery resolves against the line of work, so correspondents keep reaching the same thread in both directions across a handover, even after checkpoints are pruned.

FR-10# Requirement 10 of 10

SHALL The Hub SHALL record on the checkpoint the successor it was cut over to, in the same transaction that creates the successor, and SHALL refuse any cutover that would give a conversation a second successor, whether by the same checkpoint or another one of that conversation, whatever the timing; the refusal SHALL name the successor that holds the work. A conversation archived by hand and never handed over SHALL be refused with advice to unarchive it first.

WhyA second successor would redo finished work and fork a linear line of work. The check reads the handover record, not the lifecycle, because reopening an archived conversation is always allowed; a conditional claim and a partial unique index make two simultaneous presses produce exactly one successor.

Behaviour

Operator checkpoint and cutover

  1. UI: if a write for this conversation is already in flight, return that same promise.
  2. POST .../conversations/{id}/checkpoint; the Hub generates the checkpoint on the configured checkpoint runner (409 if none, or if one is already being written) and clears any due/final/dismissed warning.
  3. If the returned status is not `ready`, stop and show the generation error or the status.
  4. POST .../checkpoints/{checkpoint_id}/cutover.
  5. Hub: refuse if the checkpoint is not ready, if the conversation was already handed over (naming the successor), if it is archived, or if it has an unfinished run or undelivered input.
  6. Hub: add the successor and its queued checkpoint entry, archive the predecessor, then claim the checkpoint with a conditional UPDATE; on no row or an integrity error, roll back and refuse naming whoever won.
  7. Hub: commit, optionally schedule the agent when auto-continue is on, broadcast `conversation_cut_over`, return the successor id.
  8. UI: move to the successor.

Design

D1. The Hub writes the checkpoint; the agent is not asked to. Agent-written handoff files were unreachable or never written while the run reported success.

D2. The checkpoint reaches the successor as a conversation-scoped queued entry. Agent-scoped context is one file per agent and cannot carry one conversation's checkpoint.

D3. Successor first, archive second, claim last, in one transaction: a failure part-way leaves the predecessor usable.

D4. At-most-once is a fact on the checkpoint (`cut_over_to_conversation_id`), claimed by a conditional UPDATE and backed by a partial unique index per conversation, never inferred from the predecessor's lifecycle, because unarchiving is always allowed.

D5. Lineage is a column on the conversation (`lineage_id`, self by default), so delivery has no special case for the first conversation and does not need checkpoint rows.

D6. A successor title is derived from the predecessor's and marked operator-set, so the chain reads as one line in navigation and the titler does not rename it after its first acknowledgement.

Limits

  • The offer banner's cutover replaces any 409 sentence from the Hub with a generic 'finish or stop the run' message, so a refusal naming the successor is not shown on that path (AgentOutputPanel.tsx:626-630); the Checkpoint button path shows the Hub's sentence (checkpointOperationStore.ts:77-81).
  • The offer banner does not read `cut_over_to_conversation_id`, so a spent checkpoint is still offered on the archived predecessor (AgentOutputPanel.tsx:613-615).
