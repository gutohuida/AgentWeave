Summary

A tracked spec/journey.json holds a project's spec steps in order: the built-in steps, custom steps the operator pastes as Markdown at any position, and an instruction appended to any step. The Hub briefs each turn from it, so an appended instruction reaches only its own step and a custom step runs where it was placed. Edited from the project page. Roadmap slice project-steps; decided structure-steps (spec-queue/DECISIONS.md, 2026-10-08).

Problem

The step journey is one table in code (hub/hub/spec_journey.py JOURNEYS and STEP_DUTIES, by size). A project that needs a step of its own (a threat model, a data-retention check) or wants to add one line to a built-in step ('name the migration in intake') cannot: the only lever is the charter, which reaches every turn rather than the one step it is about, and instruction adherence falls as instructions grow. spec_journey's own D2 left the table to be replaced by the project's setting.

Scope

In scope

  • spec/journey.json: the project's ordered steps (built-in keys, custom steps with Markdown and sizes) and an appended instruction per step
  • Briefing, advance and the operator's step move read the project's journey; an appended instruction reaches only its own step
  • A size cap on every instruction text, refused at save
  • GET/PUT /projects/{id}/project/journey and a Spec steps section on the project page
  • The phase bar shows the project's journey, custom steps included

Non-goals

  • Reordering or removing built-in steps (acceptance is never skipped; built-ins keep their order)
  • Project-wide context injected into every step (the charter already does this)
  • A structured output field or rendering custom payload sections: an instruction names its output in its own words
  • Approval warnings for skipped custom steps (slice approval-warnings)
  • Agents editing the journey: the operator owns it

Requirements

FR-1# Requirement 1 of 9

MUST The Hub MUST read a project's spec steps from the tracked file spec/journey.json: an ordered steps list in which each entry is a built-in step (by key) or a custom step (key, title, Markdown instructions, sizes), and any entry may carry an appended instruction; with no file, every journey MUST equal today's built-in table.

WhyDecided structure-steps: a clone or a team gets the same steps; no file must change nothing.

FR-2# Requirement 2 of 9

MUST The built-in steps MUST all be present in the file in their built-in order, with their per-size membership unchanged; a custom step MAY sit at any position before, between or after them and belongs to the journey of each size it lists (small and large when it lists none).

Whymissing(), sizing and the never-skipped acceptance step assume the built-in order.

FR-3# Requirement 3 of 9

MUST A turn on a custom step MUST be briefed with the journey line, the marker [step: <key>], the step's title and Markdown, a line saying to write the result where the instructions say or, when they name no place, to choose a document section or file and tell the operator where, and the ask-to-advance protocol; advance and the operator's step move MUST enter and leave it in the file's order.

FR-4# Requirement 4 of 9

MUST An appended instruction MUST appear in its own step's briefing and in advance's instructions for that step, and in no other step's.

FR-5# Requirement 5 of 9

MUST Saving the journey MUST be refused, naming the step and the limit, when a custom step's instructions or an appended instruction exceed 2,000 characters.

WhyToday's longest duty is 1,805 characters with its advance protocol; a step briefing must stay short to be followed.

FR-6# Requirement 6 of 9

MUST The operator MUST be able to read and replace the project's steps through GET and PUT /projects/{id}/project/journey and a Spec steps section on the project page (insert a custom step by pasting Markdown at any position, append an instruction to any step, remove a custom step); the Hub MUST write spec/journey.json deterministically (stable key order, indented, LF, trailing newline).

FR-7# Requirement 7 of 9

MUST A spec/journey.json that does not parse or breaks a rule (unknown or missing built-in, built-ins out of order, a duplicate key, a custom key that is not a lowercase slug of at most 48 characters (the step column is String(48)), text over the cap) MUST be reported as a diagnostic by GET journey, and turns MUST meanwhile be briefed from the built-in table; no turn is refused for it.

WhyWarn, never gate (operator, 2026-10-08); a hand-edited file must not stop a project's spec work.

FR-8# Requirement 8 of 9

MUST A document whose recorded step is not in its project's journey for its size MUST be briefed that the step was removed and asked to call ask_user for the step to resume at; advance with no target from it MUST answer 422 step_not_in_journey naming the journey.

FR-9# Requirement 9 of 9

MUST The documents view's journey field and the phase bar MUST show the project's journey for the document's size, custom steps included, in file order.

Design

Tier 1: a feature across the journey module, three routes and the project page. No migration (the steps are a file; documents keep their step column, which already holds any string). No Hub-restart hazard; with no journey.json every project behaves exactly as today.

D1. One ordered steps list, built-ins named by key and fixed in order; custom steps anywhere. Rejected: anchored insertions (after: requirements), which have no position in the small journey where requirements is merged; and free reordering of built-ins, which breaks missing() and the never-skipped acceptance step. (night-1009-1) D2. A custom step that lists no sizes joins small and large (and unsized, briefed as large), not fix. Rejected: every size, since a fix's three-step path is the point of sizing. (night-1009-2) D3. Where an instruction's output goes is said in its own Markdown; the Hub adds one line telling the agent to follow it or choose and say where. Rejected: a structured output field, a schema and renderer for one sentence of briefing. (night-1009-3) D4. A broken hand-edited file is a diagnostic and the built-in table is used meanwhile; PUT refuses it up front. Rejected: refusing turns until the file is fixed (warn, never gate). (night-1009-4) D5. The steps are a file with their own route, not a ProjectSettings field: settings live in the database, and structure-steps put the steps in a tracked file. D6. The cap is 2,000 characters per text, above today's longest duty (1,805 with its advance protocol).
